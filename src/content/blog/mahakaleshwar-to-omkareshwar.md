The journey between **Mahakaleshwar in Ujjain** and **Omkareshwar** on the Narmada is the most travelled leg of the Madhya Pradesh Jyotirlinga circuit. Two of the twelve most sacred Shiva shrines in the country, separated by **135–140 kilometres** of Malwa plateau — a drive of roughly 3 to 3.5 hours that carries you from the grand temple city of Ujjain to the island-encircled sanctum on the Narmada.

Here is the complete route guide, covering every transport option, road condition, time estimate, and planning tip for pilgrims travelling between these two Jyotirlingas.

## Distance, Routes, and Road Conditions

**Mahakaleshwar to Omkareshwar by road covers approximately 135–140 km.** The journey crosses the Malwa plateau — flat agricultural land dotted with small towns — before descending toward the Narmada valley where Omkareshwar's island temple awaits.

There are two main route options:

**Route 1: Via Khandwa (Mhow–Khandwa Road)**
- Distance: approximately 140–160 km
- Driving time: 3.5–4 hours
- Road quality: State highway, generally good but with sections that slow during monsoon
- Best for: Travellers who want a straightforward drive and prefer the northern approach

**Route 2: Via Indore Side**
- Distance: approximately 135–150 km
- Driving time: 3–3.5 hours
- Road quality: Good, four-lane sections near Indore, then two-lane highway
- Best for: Travellers departing from Indore or those who want the marginally shorter drive

**Route recommendation:** Both roads are viable on most days. The best choice depends on current conditions — traffic, ongoing road work, or weather. Ask your driver or your hotel front desk on the morning of travel. They will know which road is running smoother that day. This is the kind of local knowledge that makes a real difference, and good dharamshala front desks make it their business to know. ## Transport Options

### Private Car with Driver (Recommended)

The most popular and practical option for families and serious pilgrims. A hired car with a driver from Ujjain costs approximately ₹2,500–3,500 for a full day (12 hours). This gives you complete flexibility: leave when you want, stop at Maheshwar or other points of interest, wait for darshan without the meter running, and return at your own pace.

The car-and-driver model also means the driver handles navigation, parking near the ghats at Omkareshwar, and any unexpected road decisions — all valuable when you are focused on the spiritual itinerary.

**Tip:** Agree the full-day rate in advance, including waiting charges. Many Ujjain dharamshalas including Mahakal Bhakta Nivas can arrange trusted drivers on request.

### Self-Drive

Very doable for experienced drivers. The roads are mostly well-maintained state highways, and the drive through the Malwa plateau is pleasant and straightforward. Key considerations:

- Fuel up before leaving Ujjain — petrol stations become sparse near the Narmada valley
- The descent toward Omkareshwar has some sharp hairpin bends — drive carefully, especially during or after rains
- Carry your driver's licence, vehicle papers, and some cash for tolls and parking
- The plateau stretches can feel long and monotonous — a co-driver is helpful

### Bus

Madhya Pradesh State Transport runs buses between Ujjain and the Khandwa/Omkareshwar route, with a change at Khandwa or Burhanpur. This is the most budget-friendly option at approximately ₹150–250 per person, but:

- Journey time extends to 5–6 hours due to stops
- You will need local transport (auto or shared jeep) from the bus stop to the Omkareshwar ghats
- Flexibility is limited — buses run on fixed schedules

Buses are practical for solo budget travellers or groups that do not mind the extra time in exchange for lower cost.

### Train + Road

There is no direct train to Omkareshwar. The nearest railway station is **Khandwa Junction (KNW)**, approximately 60 km from Omkareshwar. Several trains connect Ujjain Junction (UJN) to Khandwa:

- **Ujjain–Khandwa Passenger:** Daily, slow but budget-friendly
- **Ujjain–Bhusaval Express:** Daily, faster
- **Dayodaya Express (Ujjain–Jabalpur):** Connects through relevant stations

From Khandwa, hire an auto or shared vehicle to Omkareshwar (₹200–400). Total time via train: 2.5–4 hours train + 1.5 hours road.

## How Long Does the Journey Take?

| Mode | Approximate Time |
|---|---|
| Private car (direct) | 3–3.5 hours |
| Self-drive | 3.5–4 hours (with stops) |
| Bus (with change) | 5–6 hours |
| Train to Khandwa + road | 4–5.5 hours total |

Leave Ujjain by 7–8 AM and you arrive at Omkareshwar comfortably before midday — ideal for a relaxed darshan before the afternoon heat builds.

## The Same-Day Plan (Proven)

This plan works well for devotees who want Mahakaleshwar and Omkareshwar in one determined day:

**6:00 AM** — Wake up, quick breakfast (ask for an early packed breakfast at your Ujjain accommodation — [our front desk](/amenities/) arranges this for yatri departures)
**7:00 AM** — Depart Ujjain by car
**10:30–11:00 AM** — Arrive at Omkareshwar, cross to the island ghat
**11:00 AM** — **Omkareshwar darshan** — main island temple. Expect 20–40 minutes inside for a peaceful darshan (less crowded than Mahakaleshwar's peak hours)
**12:30 PM** — Cross the bridge or take a boat to **Mamleshwar** temple on the opposite bank — the complementary shrine that completes the Omkareshwar experience
**1:30 PM** — Lunch at a riverside restaurant near the ghats
**2:30 PM** — **Island parikrama** — the 7–10 km circumambulation of the Om-shaped island. Takes approximately 2–3 hours at a devotee's pace, with stops at the 108 Shiva temples along the route. Alternatively, if your legs need rest after the drive, take a Narmada boat ride instead — equally spiritual and easier on the knees
**5:00 PM** — Depart Omkareshwar for Ujjain
**8:00–8:30 PM** — Back in Ujjain, with time for a relaxed dinner

**For pilgrims with a Bhasma Aarti the next morning:** This plan gets you back by 9 PM, well-rested and ready for a 3 AM wake-up. The pairing of Mahakaleshwar's Bhasma Aarti followed by Omkareshwar's island walk is one of the most spiritually satisfying short pilgrimages in India.

## The Overnight Alternative

If your pace is gentler or you are travelling with elders, consider an overnight stay at Omkareshwar:

- **Afternoon:** Arrive, complete darshan at both Omkareshwar and Mamleshwar
- **Evening:** **Narmada aarti** on the ghats — hundreds of earthen lamps floating on the river, devotional songs, and the sound of bells across the water. Far more intimate than the formal aartis of Ujjain
- **Next morning:** Dawn parikrama around the island in the cool morning air, before the heat and crowds arrive
- **Late morning:** Drive back to Ujjain, arriving by early afternoon

The trade is one extra hotel night versus a long day on the road. For most families and senior pilgrims, the overnight wins on comfort alone.

## What to See at Omkareshwar

### The Main Temple (Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga)
The main Jyotirlinga temple sits on the island, surrounded by 108 smaller Shiva shrines that make the parikrama possible. The lingam itself is a natural stone form, installed in a sanctum that sees a steady but manageable flow of devotees.

### Mamleshwar Temple
On the opposite bank of the Narmada, Mamleshwar is considered the complementary half of the Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga — tradition holds that both temples together constitute the complete shrine. Many devotees consider Mamleshwar the quieter and more peaceful of the two, and crossing the river to visit it is an essential part of the full experience.

### The Island Parikrama
The circumambulation of the island — approximately 7–10 km depending on the exact route — is a pilgrimage in itself. Stone steps, small shrines, river views, and the constant presence of the Narmada make this one of the most spiritually nourishing walks in central India. Most devotees complete it in 2–3 hours with normal walking pace and shrine stops.

### Narmada Ghats and Aarti
The ghats on the riverbank at Omkareshwar are where the evening aarti takes place. The Narmada aarti is more intimate than Ujjain's — fewer people, more personal, the lamps floating on the sacred river water. It is a deeply moving experience, especially on full-moon nights.

## How to Combine Omkareshwar with Maheshwar

The Omkareshwar–Maheshwar–Ujjain triangle is the most complete short spiritual itinerary in Madhya Pradesh. Here is how to layer them:

**Day 1 — Ujjain:** Arrive, Mahakaleshwar darshan, evening aarti. Stay near the temple — [our rooms](/rooms/).

**Day 2 — Ujjain to Maheshwar:** Bhasma Aarti at dawn, then drive to Maheshwar (50 km, ~1.5 hours). Explore Ahilya Fort, Narmada ghats, and the handloom saree markets. Afternoon at leisure. Overnight at Maheshwar or Mandu.

**Day 3 — Maheshwar to Omkareshwar:** Short drive to Omkareshwar (50 km, ~1.5 hours). Complete darshan, parikrama, and Mamleshwar. Return to Ujjain in the evening — or extend to Ujjain via Mandu (add one more night).

This four-day plan covers the most sacred sites, the most beautiful heritage, and the most atmospheric riverside experience in Madhya Pradesh. It is the Malwa circuit in its most complete form.

## Planning Tips

- **Best season:** October through March. The plateau is comfortable for walking, and the Narmada is clear. Monsoon (July–September) makes the river dramatic but the parikrama slippery.
- **Weekday advantage:** Omkareshwar is significantly quieter on weekdays. Kartik Purnima draws major crowds — plan accordingly.
- **Luggage:** If doing a same-day trip, leave heavy bags at your Ujjain room — day-packs only. We store luggage for departing guests at no extra charge. [Ask the front desk](/contact/).
- **Cash:** Ticket counters, boat operators, and small shrines prefer cash. Network on the island can be patchy.
- **Bhasma Aarti + Omkareshwar pairing:** Do the Bhasma Aarti on Day 1 morning in Ujjain, rest, then Omkareshwar on Day 2 — the spiritual energy compounds beautifully across the two days.
