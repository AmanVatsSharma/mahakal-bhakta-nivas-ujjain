# Mahakaleshwar Aarti Timings: All 5 Daily Aartis with Seasonal Schedule & Tips

The Mahakaleshwar Temple in Ujjain is one of the few temples in India where you can witness **five aartis every single day**. Each aarti has its own character, its own crowd, and its own spiritual significance. Understanding the schedule — and which aartis to prioritize — is the key to making the most of your visit.

This guide covers all five aartis with exact timings, seasonal variations, crowd levels, and practical tips for each one.

## The Five Daily Aartis of Mahakaleshwar

### 1. Bhasma Aarti — 4:00 AM

**Timing:** 4:00 AM daily (year-round, with minor seasonal adjustments)
**Duration:** 30–45 minutes
**Significance:** The most sacred and unique aarti at Mahakaleshwar. The Jyotirlinga is bathed with sacred ash (bhasma) collected from homa fires, accompanied by powerful Vedic mantras. This is the aarti that draws pilgrims from across the world.

**Crowd level:** Extremely high. Bhasma Aarti passes are limited and allocated in advance. Without a pass, you can still attend the general darshan queue, but the experience is different.

**Tips for Bhasma Aarti:**
- Pre-book passes online — they sell out days in advance, especially during Shivaratri and festivals
- Arrive at the temple by **3:00 AM** at the latest. The queue starts forming at 2:30 AM.
- Carry your original photo ID (Aadhaar, passport, driving license) — it is verified at entry
- Wear a warm shawl or jacket — the temple interior is cold in the early morning, especially in winter
- Wear comfortable socks or footwear that is easy to remove — you will walk barefoot on cold stone
- Do not bring phones inside the Bhasma Aarti area if they are restricted — check with the temple office
- Photography is **strictly prohibited** during Bhasma Aarti

### 2. Morning Aarti — 7:00 AM

**Timing:** 7:00 AM daily
**Duration:** 15–20 minutes
**Significance:** The first aarti after the temple opens for the day. The morning light entering the temple creates a beautiful atmosphere. This aarti is accessible to all — no pass required.

**Crowd level:** Moderate to high. Devotees who attend Bhasma Aarti often stay for the morning aarti as well, so the crowd is already present.

**Tips for Morning Aarti:**
- Arrive by 6:30 AM for a good position in the queue
- The morning aarti is a great option if you could not get Bhasma Aarti passes
- The combination of Bhasma Aarti + morning aarti is the most popular morning schedule
- After the aarti, collect prasadam (sugar crystals or peda)

### 3. Noon Aarti — 11:00 AM

**Timing:** 11:00 AM daily
**Duration:** 15–20 minutes
**Significance:** The midday aarti is less crowded and more intimate. It is ideal for devotees who prefer a quieter darshan experience. The temple is still open but the morning rush has dissipated.

**Crowd level:** Low to moderate. One of the best times to visit for a peaceful darshan.

**Tips for Noon Aarti:**
- Ideal for families with children or elderly members who cannot handle crowds
- You can combine the noon aarti with a visit to the nearby Bade Ganesh Temple
- The weather is warm at this time — carry water
- Shop for prasadam and religious items in the market after darshan

### 4. Sandhya Aarti (Evening Aarti) — 6:30 PM

**Timing:** 6:30 PM daily (with minor seasonal variation)
**Duration:** 20–30 minutes
**Significance:** The evening aarti is one of the most spectacular. As the sun sets, the temple is illuminated with lamps, the priests chant the evening mantras, and the atmosphere is deeply moving. The Sandhya Aarti at Mahakaleshwar is comparable to the famous Ganga aarti in Varanasi.

**Crowd level:** High. This is one of the most popular aartis — second only to Bhasma Aarti.

**Tips for Sandhya Aarti:**
- Arrive by 6:00 PM for a good position
- The aarti coincides with the time when the temple looks most beautiful — the lamps against the evening sky
- Stay for the full aarti — do not rush through
- After the aarti, visit the nearby ghats on the Shipra River for a peaceful evening

### 5. Night Aarti — 8:30 PM

**Timing:** 8:30 PM daily
**Duration:** 15–20 minutes
**Significance:** The night aarti is the last ritual of the day before the temple closes. It is quieter and more contemplative. The temple's night illumination creates a serene, almost ethereal atmosphere.

**Crowd level:** Low to moderate. Fewer visitors attend the night aarti, which makes it special.

**Tips for Night Aarti:**
- Ideal for devotees who want a final, intimate darshan before resting
- The temple closes shortly after the night aarti — check closing times
- The area around the temple is quieter at night, which adds to the spiritual atmosphere
- If you are staying at a nearby dharamshala, you can walk back after the aarti

## Seasonal Timing Variations

### Winter (October–February)

Aarti timings shift slightly due to earlier sunsets.

| Aarti | Winter Timing |
|---|---|
| Bhasma Aarti | 4:00 AM (no change) |
| Morning Aarti | 7:00 AM (no change) |
| Noon Aarti | 11:00 AM (no change) |
| Sandhya Aarti | 5:30 PM |
| Night Aarti | 8:00 PM |

**Tip:** Winter days are shorter, so the gap between Sandhya Aarti and Night Aarti is shorter. Plan accordingly.

### Summer (March–June)

Longer days mean later evening aartis.

| Aarti | Summer Timing |
|---|---|
| Bhasma Aarti | 4:00 AM (no change) |
| Morning Aarti | 7:00 AM (no change) |
| Noon Aarti | 11:00 AM (no change) |
| Sandhya Aarti | 7:00 PM |
| Night Aarti | 9:00 PM |

**Tip:** The noon aarti in summer is particularly hot. If you attend, carry water and sun protection.

### Monsoon (July–September)

Timings revert to winter schedule as days shorten. Rain can make walking to the temple uncomfortable — carry an umbrella and wear waterproof footwear that is easy to remove.

## Which Aartis Should You Prioritize?

### If You Have One Day in Ujjain:

**Best combination:** Bhasma Aarti (4 AM) + Sandhya Aarti (6:30 PM)

This gives you the most sacred aarti experience in the morning and the most beautiful aarti experience in the evening. It covers the full spiritual arc of a day at Mahakaleshwar.

### If You Have Two Days:

**Day 1:** Bhasma Aarti + Morning Aarti + Noon Aarti
**Day 2:** Sandhya Aarti + Night Aarti

This is the complete Mahakaleshwar experience — every aarti, every atmosphere.

### If Bhasma Aarti Passes Are Unavailable:

**Best combination:** Morning Aarti + Sandhya Aarti + Night Aarti

You will still experience the full range of the temple's daily rhythm. The morning aarti gives you the fresh-start energy, the Sandhya Aarti gives you the spectacle, and the night aarti gives you the peace.

### For Elderly or Crowd-Sensitive Visitors:

**Best combination:** Noon Aarti + Night Aarti

The noon aarti is quiet, and the night aarti is peaceful. You can visit the temple twice without the intense crowds of Bhasma or Sandhya aartis.

## Crowd Levels: When to Visit

| Aarti | Crowd Level | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Bhasma Aarti | Very High | Devotees who want the most sacred experience |
| Morning Aarti | High | General darshan, early birds |
| Noon Aarti | Low–Moderate | Peaceful visit, families, elderly |
| Sandhya Aarti | High | Spectacle, photography (outside sanctum) |
| Night Aarti | Low–Moderate | Quiet experience, last-minute darshan |

## Practical Tips Across All Aartis

1. **Arrive early.** The earlier you arrive, the better your position in the queue. Even for low-crowd aartis, arriving 15 minutes early is worth it.
2. **Keep your phone charged and on silent.** You will need it for photos and emergencies.
3. **Carry minimal belongings.** A small pouch with ID, cash, and phone is enough.
4. **Do not bring food or water bottles inside.** Eat and drink before entering.
5. **Dress conservatively.** The dress code applies to all aartis, not just Bhasma.
6. **Remove shoes before entering.** The shoe-rack area is outside the gate — plan for a few extra minutes.
7. **Respect the queue.** Do not try to cut in line. The temple staff are strict about queue discipline.
8. **Stay for prasadam.** After every aarti, priests distribute prasadam. Accept it with both hands as a sign of respect.

## The Best Combination for First-Time Visitors

If you are visiting Mahakaleshwar for the first time, we recommend this schedule:

**Day 1 Morning:** Bhasma Aarti (4 AM) → Morning Aarti (7 AM) → Return to room for rest
**Day 1 Evening:** Sandhya Aarti (6:30 PM)
**Day 2 Morning:** Morning Aarti (7 AM) or Noon Aarti (11 AM)
**Day 2 Evening:** Night Aarti (8:30 PM) → Depart

This gives you four aartis over two days — enough to feel the full rhythm of Mahakaleshwar without exhaustion.

To make this schedule work, stay within walking distance of the temple. [Mahakal Bhakta Nivas](/rooms/) is close enough that you can walk to the temple at 3:30 AM for Bhasma Aarti and return to rest afterward.

## FAQs

**Q1: At what time is Bhasma Aarti at Mahakaleshwar?**
Bhasma Aarti begins at 4:00 AM daily. Queue entry starts around 2:30–3:00 AM. Pre-book passes online as they sell out fast.

**Q2: Do I need a pass for all aartis at Mahakaleshwar?**
No. Only Bhasma Aarti requires a pre-booked pass. The morning, noon, Sandhya, and night aartis are open to all devotees through the general queue.

**Q3: Which is the best aarti to attend at Mahakaleshwar?**
Bhasma Aarti is the most spiritually significant and unique to Mahakaleshwar. Sandhya Aarti is the most visually spectacular. For a first-time visitor, try to attend both.

**Q4: Can I attend all five aartis in one day?**
Yes, but it is physically demanding. Bhasma (4 AM) → Morning (7 AM) → Noon (11 AM) → Sandhya (6:30 PM) → Night (8:30 PM) covers every aarti, but the noon and evening gaps are long. Most devotees spread aartis over 2–3 days.

**Q5: Are aarti timings different during festivals?**
During Shivaratri and other major festivals, the schedule may change. Additional aartis may be added. Check the temple's official announcement closer to your visit dates.
