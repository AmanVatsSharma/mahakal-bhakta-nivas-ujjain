The aartis are the heartbeat of Mahakaleshwar — the Lord of Time is honoured through the entire day, from before dawn to past midnight. If you are planning which ones to attend, here is the complete timing list, what each aarti involves, and how attendance works.

## The Complete Aarti & Puja Schedule

| Aarti / Ritual | Time | Booking |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhasma Aarti** | 4:00 AM – 6:00 AM | **Mandatory** — official portal |
| Morning darshan | ~6:00 AM onwards | Free, no pass |
| **Mahabhog (midday) puja** | ~12:00 PM | Optional paid participation |
| **Sandhya (evening) aarti** | 7:00 PM | Pass bookable; general darshan open |
| **Shayan (night) aarti** | 11:00 PM | Pass bookable |

Timings are approximate, and festival days (especially **Mahashivratri** and the **Shravan** month) shift everything earlier and longer. Check the notice section of the [official portal](https://www.shrimahakaleshwar.mp.gov.in/) the day before you go.

## Bhasma Aarti (4:00–6:00 AM) — The Crown Ritual

The only aarti of its kind among all twelve Jyotirlingas: sacred ash is offered to Mahakal as the day begins. Attendance requires advance booking, original photo ID, and traditional clothing — devotees report by ~3:00–3:30 AM. Demand is extreme; slots open monthly and vanish in minutes.

Full process, fees and backup plans: [How to Book Bhasma Aarti at Mahakaleshwar](/blog/how-to-book-bhasma-aarti-mahakaleshwar/)

## Mahabhog Puja (~12:00 PM)

At midday the Lord is offered a grand food aarti (mahabhog). Portions of the bhog can be booked/collected via the portal's puja services — a meaningful option for families dedicating an offering. The temple is at its busiest around this window, so plan darshan before or after.

## Sandhya Aarti (7:00 PM) — The Accessible Classic

As lamps are lit and the evening bells resound, the sanctum takes on its most photographed atmosphere. Passes can be booked online, but in normal periods general darshan flows alongside — making this **the best aarti for travellers without Bhasma passes**. Arrive by 6:15–6:30 PM for a good spot.

Pro tip: pair it with a Mahakal Lok corridor walk afterwards — the evening lighting is spectacular ([guide here](/blog/mahakal-lok-corridor-guide/)).

## Shayan Aarti (11:00 PM) — The Serene Finale

The Lord is ritually put to rest for the night. Far fewer devotees stay this late, so Shayan Aarti rewards night-owls and those on longer stays with an unusually intimate temple atmosphere. The temple closes after it until the 4 AM cycle begins again.

## Planning Your Day Around the Aartis

A rhythm that works for most pilgrims on a one-day visit:

- **6:30 AM** — darshan (calm window after Bhasma Aarti)
- **8:00 AM** — breakfast back at your dharamshala
- **Daytime** — nearby temples (Kal Bhairav, Harsiddhi), rest
- **6:15 PM** — return for Sandhya Aarti + Mahakal Lok in the evening
- **Optional 10:30 PM** — return for Shayan Aarti

## Aarti-Day Practicalities

- Traditional attire; no leather; phones deposited outside
- Separate seating sections for men and women at Bhasma Aarti
- Festival days: expect multi-hour queues — see our [darshan guide](/blog/mahakaleshwar-darshan-guide/) for crowd strategy
