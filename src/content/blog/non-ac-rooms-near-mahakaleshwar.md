## Why October to February Is Non-AC Season in Ujjain

Ujjain has a real winter. From mid-October the nights cool down, and by December and January the early mornings are genuinely cold — the kind of cold where a 4 AM walk to Bhasma Aarti needs a shawl, not a cooling debate. In this window an air conditioner sits idle while you still pay a premium for it at most temple-quarter stays.

That arithmetic is what makes a **budget room near Mahakaleshwar** in the non-AC category the sensible default for the season:

- **Night temperatures are comfortable to cool.** A ceiling fan on low is usually enough; on many nights you will switch it off entirely.
- **You are outdoors at the hours that matter.** Bhasma Aarti at dawn, Somvar queues, evening aarti at Ram Ghat — the room is for resting, and winter rest needs no chillers.
- **The saving compounds for families.** On a 3-bed or 4-bed room, the difference between the AC and non-AC versions covers a full pure veg dinner for the family at our own kitchen.

## Fans, Ventilation and 24/7 Power Backup

A good **dharamshala non AC** room is built around airflow, not just a fan bolted to the ceiling. Before you book any non-AC room anywhere in Ujjain, check three things:

1. **Window placement.** Cross-ventilation from two directions beats a single sealed window every time.
2. **Fan quality.** A smooth multi-speed fan absorbs the warm spells that sneak into October and March.
3. **Power backup.** This is the one that separates a good stay from a sweaty night. Outages happen year-round, and a fan without power is just decoration. Our rooms run on **24/7 power backup**, so the fan keeps turning even when the streetlights do not.

That third point is why "non-AC" at a well-run dharamshala is not the gamble it might be at a cut-price lodge. A fan with backup, a ventilated room and a cool-season calendar is a comfortable room, full stop.

## When You Should Take AC Anyway — Honest Advice

**April to June, take AC.** No clever framing changes this: day temperatures in Ujjain cross 40°C, nights stay hot, and a non-AC room in that window means arriving at evening darshan already drained. If your dates fall in the summer quarter, read our guide to [AC rooms near Mahakaleshwar Temple](/blog/ac-rooms-near-mahakaleshwar-temple/) — our 2 Bed AC at ₹1,650 is the entry point, and the same fixed-tariff rules apply.

The shoulder months need judgment:

- **March** — warm afternoons, mostly fine nights. Non-AC works for most travellers, but heat-sensitive elders may prefer AC.
- **July to September (monsoon)** — humid rather than hot. Non-AC with a fan suits most people; families with infants sometimes prefer AC for drier comfort.
- **October and November** — the sweet spot: post-monsoon clarity, cool evenings, and non-AC comfortably wins the math.

If you are torn between two room types, tell us your dates and who is travelling. Elders, infants and anyone with heat sensitivity tilt the answer toward AC regardless of season — we would rather lose a price difference than your comfort.
