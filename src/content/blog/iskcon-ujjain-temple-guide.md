Between the cosmic weight of Mahakal and the mythology of the ghats, Ujjain keeps one corner of pure, uncomplicated joy. **ISKCON Ujjain** — the **Sri Sri Radha Madan Mohan temple** — stands barely a kilometre or two from the Mahakal gate, and it offers what the bigger landmarks cannot always manage: **silence, sweetness and a plate of hot prasad**. After two crowded darshan mornings, many pilgrims tell us this was the hour their bodies finally relaxed.

We recommend it without hesitation to guests at our dharamshala on Gopal Mandir Road — especially families with tired children, elders who need a calmer pace, and anyone whose devotion runs to music rather than queues.

## A Temple the City Built Together

The temple of **Sri Sri Radha Madan Mohan** rose with the **support of the Maheshwari community** of Ujjain — a merchant community of the city that backed the construction, as it has backed so much of the city's religious infrastructure across centuries. That origin still shapes the feel of the place: it is a neighbourhood temple of international lineage, spotlessly kept, unhurried, and run with the quiet pride of people who helped build it.

The deities are the heart of it — **Radha and Madan Mohan**, Krishna in his most graceful form, dressed fresh through the day, with garlands, flute and the luminous pastel palette ISKCON altars are loved for. For Vaishnava pilgrims doing a Krishna circuit of Ujjain, this altar pairs naturally with **Sandipani Ashram**, where the same Krishna sat as a student — the child of the ashram and the god of the altar, one deity growing up across two corners of the city.

And the temple's signature gift is the sound. The **kirtans** here are everything you hope for: harmonium, mridanga, a hall of voices, and melodies simple enough that by the second refrain you are singing too, whatever your background. For elders especially, an hour of seated kirtan is devotion without exhaustion — no queue, no climbing, no rush; just music, warmth and the smell of incense.

## What You Will Find

- **The altar of Radha Madan Mohan** — beautifully maintained, with darshan arranged so even a short visit feels complete.
- **Regular kirtans** — morning and evening sessions; the **evening aarti with kirtan** is the one to plan around, when the hall fills and the singing carries.
- **Prasad and meals** — prasad distribution through the day and full **pure veg meals**; the famous **Sunday feast** program draws families from across the city.
- **Clean and calm surroundings** — the single most repeated phrase in what visitors say about this temple, and deservedly so. Marble floors, shaded quiet, none of the jostle of the old-city shrines.
- **A book and gift counter** — translations of the Bhagavad Gita, incense, and small gifts, if you want to carry something home.

## Timings and the Best Slot

**ISKCON temple Ujjain timing** follows the standard ISKCON rhythm rather than the old-city pattern:

- **Mangala aarti** — very early morning, before most pilgrims are awake; serene if you are up for it.
- **Daytime darshan** — with a midday break around the deity rest hours.
- **Evening aarti and kirtan** — the recommended slot: the singing is fullest and the atmosphere warmest.
- **Sunday programs** — the weekly high point, ending in the Sunday feast; arrive early for seating.

As everywhere in a temple town, festival calendars — **Janmashtami above all** — expand everything. On Krishna Janmashtami the temple becomes a city-wide destination: the altar is redecorated through the day, kirtan runs for hours, and at midnight — the hour of the birth — the hall overflows with singing and the maha-aarti fills the courtyard. Arrive well before midnight if you want to be inside rather than at the gate. **Verify timings locally** on the day you visit; the temple noticeboard and phone line are reliable.

## How to Reach From the Mahakaleshwar Temple Quarter

- **Distance**: about **1 to 2 km** from the Mahakal gate.
- **On foot**: 10 to 15 minutes — an easy, flat walk suitable for elders.
- **By e-rickshaw**: a few minutes; the drivers know it as ISKCON.
- **In your itinerary**: the classic pairing is a **late-afternoon kirtan followed by the evening aarti at Mahakaleshwar**, or an evening here after a heavy morning of Bhasma Aarti and Harsiddhi. ## Tips for Visitors

- **Dress modestly** — the standard temple rule; ISKCON temples are a little stricter than most about it.
- **Shoes and bags** — racks at the entrance; travel light as always.
- **Sit for one full kirtan** — the instinct is to visit and leave; resist it. Ten minutes of sitting changes the experience entirely.
- **Sunday planning** — the feast is open to visitors; a small donation is the custom for meals.
- **Photography** — the hall is usually generous, but the altar rules vary by hour; look for the signs or simply ask.
