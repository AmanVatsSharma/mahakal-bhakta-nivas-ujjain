The Maha Mrityunjay Temple in Ujjain holds a unique place among the sacred sites of the city. Dedicated to Lord Shiva in his form as the conqueror of death, this temple draws thousands of devotees every year who seek blessings for health, longevity, and spiritual upliftment. Whether you are visiting Ujjain for the Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga darshan or making a dedicated trip to this shrine, here is everything you need to plan your visit.

## What Is the Maha Mrityunjay Temple?

The Maha Mrityunjay Temple is devoted to Lord Shiva as **Maha Mrityunjaya** — the one who has triumphed over death. The core of devotion here is the **Maha Mrityunjaya Mantra**, one of the most ancient and powerful mantras in Hinduism:

> "Om Tryambakam Yajamahe, Sugandhim Pushti Vardhanam, Urvarukamiva Bandhanan, Mrityor Mukshiya Maamritat."

Devotees chant this mantra to seek protection from illness, accidents, and premature death. The temple provides a deeply spiritual atmosphere where this mantra is chanted continuously, especially during **Maha Mrityunjaya Anushthan** (group recitation ceremonies).

## Historical and Spiritual Significance

Ujjain, historically known as **Avantika**, has been a center of Shaivism for over two millennia. Ancient texts describe it as one of the seven **Moksha Puris** — cities where salvation can be attained. The Maha Mrityunjay Temple sits within this sacred geography, close to the Kshipra River and the Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga.

Legend says that the demon **Mrikandu** performed intense penance here to ward off death, and Lord Shiva appeared as the Maha Mrityunjaya, granting him and his wife **Marudmati** a long and healthy life. Their son **Markandeya** became the famous sage who, by reciting the Mrityunjaya Mantra, defeated Yama (the god of death) himself.

This mythic backdrop makes the temple a place of profound hope, particularly for those suffering from serious illness or performing rituals for the long life of loved ones.

## Temple Timings

The temple follows a structured daily schedule:

- **Morning Opening**: 5:00 AM
- **Morning Aarti**: 7:00 AM
- **General Darshan**: 8:00 AM – 12:00 PM
- **Afternoon Closing**: 1:00 PM
- **Evening Reopening**: 4:00 PM
- **Evening Aarti**: 7:30 PM
- **Night Closing**: 9:00 PM

Timings may shift slightly during festivals like **Maha Shivratri**, **Shravan month**, and **Kumbh Mela**. Check local updates before traveling.

## How to Reach the Temple

### By Train
Ujjain Junction is well connected to major cities. From the station:

- **Auto-rickshaw**: 15-minute ride, approximately INR 100–150
- **Local taxi**: INR 200–300 to the temple area

### By Bus
The Ujjain Bus Stand is about 4 km from the temple. City buses and shared autos ply regularly.

### By Car/Taxi
If arriving from **Indore** (55 km), the drive takes roughly 1.5 hours. From **Bhopal** (180 km), it is about 3.5 hours. Paid parking is available near the temple complex on a first-come, first-served basis.

### On Foot from Mahakaleshwar Temple
The Maha Mrityunjay Temple is within walking distance of the Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga. Many pilgrims visit both temples in the same day. Plan at least **30–45 minutes** to walk between them comfortably.

## Things to Know Before You Visit

- **Dress code**: Modest Indian clothing recommended. Avoid shorts, sleeveless tops, and revealing attire.
- **Mobile phones**: Most temples in Ujjain have about phones inside the garbhagriha (sanctum). Use **cloak room facilities** at the entrance.
- **Footwear**: There is a designated shoe-shed near the entrance; carry a pair of clean socks if you prefer.
- **Best time to visit**: Early morning on a weekday. Crowds are manageable before 10 AM.
- **Accessibility**: The temple has steps at the entrance, which can be challenging for elderly visitors. Carry a walking stick or seek assistance from volunteers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**1. Is there an entry fee for Maha Mrityunjay Temple?**
No, general darshan is free. However, special poojas and anushthans have nominal fees.

**2. Can we take photographs inside the temple?**
Photography is generally not permitted inside the main shrine. Respect the rules and keep devices in the cloak room.

**3. What is the best day to visit for Mrityunjay Mantra recitation?**
Mondays (Somvar) and **Amavasya** (new moon) days are considered especially auspicious for Mrityunjay Jaap.

**4. Is the temple wheelchair accessible?**
The temple has some steps at the entrance. Call ahead or ask volunteers for assistance if you have mobility needs.

**5. Can we combine this visit with Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga?**
Absolutely. Both temples are within 1 km of each other. Many pilgrims visit Mahakaleshwar first, then walk to Maha Mrityunjay Temple for a quieter spiritual experience.

**6. Are there accommodation options nearby?**
Yes. We provide clean, well-maintained rooms for every budget — from affordable non-AC stays to premium AC rooms. All are located within a short auto ride of the temple.

**7. Is the temple open during festivals?**
Yes. During Mahashivratri, Shravan, and Kumbh Mela, the temple remains open for extended hours. Expect larger crowds and plan accordingly.

**8. Can we book Mrityunjay Anushthan online?**
Some priests and temple trusts offer online or phone booking. Walk-in bookings are also accepted but may have long waits during peak times.
