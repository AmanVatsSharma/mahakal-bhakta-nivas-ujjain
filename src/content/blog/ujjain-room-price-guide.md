## Typical Price Bands in the Temple Quarter

These are **typical price bands in the temple quarter as of 2026 — verify current rates**, because Ujjain is a demand-driven pilgrimage market and individual properties vary widely within each band:

| Band | Nightly rate | What it typically covers |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | ₹800–1,500 | Non-AC doubles, basic dharamshala rooms, some shared-bathroom options at the bottom |
| Mid-range | ₹1,500–2,500 | AC doubles and triples, better linen, attached bathrooms, often in-house food |
| Premium | ₹2,500–4,500 | Larger AC rooms, suites, hotels with services and formal front desks |

Three observations before you map your budget onto this table:

- **Position costs more than polish.** In the temple quarter, a modest room 200 m from the gate can honestly outprice a shinier room two kilometres out. You are paying for walkability, and it is usually worth paying for.
- **Dharamshalas and hotels overlap.** The bands are about room quality, not property type — a well-run dharamshala suite sits in the premium band, and a budget hotel often competes in the budget band.
- **The cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest stay.** A ₹900 room with paid extras and transport costs can exceed a ₹1,400 room that includes parking, breakfast-level tea and a kitchen.

## What Moves Room Rent in Ujjain

A **room rent in Ujjain** is not a fixed law of nature — it breathes with the religious calendar. The demand curve is predictable, so learn it once and book accordingly:

1. **Somvars (Mondays).** The Mahadev day of the week. Sunday nights before a Monday run hot year-round.
2. **Shravan.** The whole month is peak, with the first and last Somvar, Hariyali Amavasya and every intervening Monday at the very top of the curve.
3. **Mahashivratri.** The single biggest temple event of the year — expect the tightest rooms and the longest lead times.
4. **Amavasyas and festival days** such as Nag Panchami and Kartik Purnima, which pull snaan crowds to the Shipra.
5. **Weekends and long weekends**, when leisure travellers stack onto the pilgrim flow.

The practical rule: **book 2–4 weeks out for ordinary dates and 4–6 weeks out for festival dates** — and treat any quoted price you receive during peak windows with the hidden-charge checklist below.

## Hidden Charges: The Five Questions to Ask

Most "the room was more expensive than advertised" stories trace back to five questions nobody asked. Ask them before you pay, anywhere in Ujjain:

1. **"Is this the all-inclusive total?"** — Extra-person charges on a third or fourth bed are the classic silent add-on.
2. **"When is hot water available?"** — A room that heats water only in narrow windows costs you comfort, and sometimes a paid upgrade to get it.
3. **"What does early check-in or late check-out cost?"** — After a night train, a 6 AM check-in matters more than any amenity in the listing.
4. **"Is parking included?"** — In the temple quarter, parking is real money; free, on-site parking for cars and bikes is a genuine differentiator.
5. **"Is the kitchen in-house, and what does a meal cost?"** — Food spend is a room-price multiplier: three restaurant meals a day for a family can equal half the room tariff.

Get the final number in writing — on WhatsApp is fine. A tariff that survives being written down is a tariff you can budget against.
