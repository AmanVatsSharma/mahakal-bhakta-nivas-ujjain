## Planning Your Darshan Visit

The best time to visit Mahakaleshwar is between October and March, when Ujjain's heat eases and the temple atmosphere is most comfortable. July–September (Sawan month) sees heavy crowds but the spiritual energy is extraordinary — plan extra time for queues. Avoid Mahashivratri if you want a peaceful experience; the city swells beyond capacity.

## The Five Entry Gates

Mahakaleshwar Temple has five main entry gates (Dwar), each serving a different purpose:

- **Gate 1 (Main Gate / Singh Dwar):** The primary entry for general darshan queues. This is where most visitors enter. It opens at 4:00 AM and closes at 11:00 PM.
- **Gate 2 (Near the Nandi):** Used for special darshan and VIP access. Smaller and less crowded, but requires a special pass.
- **Gate 3 (Mahakal Lok Side):** Connects the temple directly to the Mahakal Lok corridor. Useful if you are doing a combined walk.
- **Gate 4 (Near Ram Ghat):** Used during festivals and for overflow crowds. Opens during peak periods.
- **Gate 5 (Administrative / Procession Gate):** Reserved for temple processions and special occasions — not for general darshan.

For regular visitors, **Gate 1 (Singh Dwar)** is your entry point. Arrive knowing which queue to join — there are separate lines for general darshan, special darshan, and Bhasma Aarti ticket holders.

## Queue System

The temple uses a straightforward queue system:

1. **General Darshan Queue** — Free entry, longest wait. Moves steadily on normal days.
2. **Special Darshan Queue (Paid)** — ₹51–₹201 depending on the category. Shorter wait, priority access. Available on a first-come-first-served basis at the special darshan counter near Gate 1.
3. **Bhasma Aarti Queue** — Requires advance online booking. You get a dedicated entry window and assigned seating for the ritual.

On festival days, the temple management activates an electronic token system. Check the official Ujjain Temple website or our front desk for the latest system before you go — the process changes occasionally.

## Morning vs Evening Darshan

Both have their charm. Morning darshan (6:00–11:00 AM) is quieter, the priests are more relaxed, and the light through the temple dome creates a beautiful atmosphere in the sanctum. Evening darshan (4:00–9:00 PM) comes alive with the Sandhya Aarti at 7:00 PM — the conch, lamps, and chants are the highlight for most first-time visitors.

If you can only pick one, **evening darshan with the aarti** is the more memorable experience. But if you want the temple to yourself, come at 6:15 AM on a weekday.

## Dress Code Enforcement

The dress code is taken seriously. Men in shorts, tank tops, or sleeveless shirts will be turned away at the gate. Women in short-length kurtas or jeans may be asked to drape a dupatta or borrow a shawl from the counter. There is a small cloth rental shop near the Singh Dwar where you can pick up a dhoti or dupatta for a nominal fee if needed.

The rule is simple: dress as you would for a temple visit in your own family — modest, respectful, covered shoulders and knees.

## Mahakal Lok Corridor Route

The Mahakal Lok is a beautifully designed corridor with 108 Shiva sculptures, murals depicting episodes from the Shiva Purana, and landscaped gardens. It connects the temple to the broader temple complex and is a walk worth taking before or after darshan.

**Route:** Enter from Gate 3 (the Mahakal Lok side gate of the temple), walk the sculpted corridor, and exit near the temple tank. The walk takes 20–30 minutes at a relaxed pace. The corridor is illuminated beautifully in the evening — plan your return darshan so you can walk it at night.

## Pre-Darshan Preparation

A well-prepared start makes the entire darshan smoother. If you are heading out for an early morning darshan — especially the Bhasma Aarti at 4:00 AM — follow this step-by-step routine:

**The Night Before:**
- Lay out your temple clothes: a clean dhoti or full-length pant, a simple shirt, and a shawl if the morning is cold. Do this before you sleep so you are not fumbling in the dark.
- Pack a small cloth bag with your ID, some cash, and a small offering packet (flowers or prasad, available at local shops near the temple).
- Charge your phone and set two alarms — one for your wake-up and a backup 15 minutes later. You do not want to miss the 4:00 AM slot because of a dead phone.
- Confirm your cloakroom locker reservation if you booked one in advance, or note the location of the nearest cloakroom near Gate 1.
- Drink plenty of water the evening before and avoid heavy, spicy meals at dinner. A light meal of khichdi or plain rice keeps you comfortable through the pre-dawn hours.

**The Morning Of:**
- Wake up at least 90 minutes before your planned darshan time. If Bhasma Aarti starts at 4:00 AM, be up by 2:30 AM.
- Freshen up quickly and dress in your temple clothes. If you are staying at [Mahakal Bhakta Nivas](/rooms/), hot water is available in the early mornings for a quick wash.
- Walk to the temple calmly. The walk from our property takes under 10 minutes, and the pre-dawn streets of Ujjain are quiet and peaceful — a perfect transition into temple mood.
- Deposit your phone, camera, and leather items at the cloakroom near the entry. Have your small cash ready for the locker fee.
- Join the correct queue: the general darshan line for regular visitors, or the Bhasma Aarti line if you have a booking. Do not hesitate to ask the temple staff which line to join — they will direct you immediately.
- Once inside, walk through the corridors with patience. The path to the sanctum goes through several halls and smaller shrines — this walk is part of the darshan experience.

A calm, prepared start sets the tone for everything that follows. Most visitors who arrive flustered and rushed find that the anxiety stays with them inside the sanctum. Arrive early, arrive prepared, and the darshan becomes something you carry with you long after you leave.

## Understanding the Queue System in Detail

The queue system at Mahakaleshwar Temple is designed to handle thousands of devotees daily while maintaining order and sanctity. Understanding it before you arrive saves time and confusion.

**General Darshan Queue (Free)**

This is the standard queue accessible to everyone. There are no tickets required — just walk up to Gate 1 (Singh Dwar) and join the line marked "General Darshan." On a normal weekday, the queue moves in waves. There may be a 15 to 30 minute wait, but the movement is steady. On Sundays and festival days, expect 45 to 90 minutes.

**How to identify it:** Look for the longest line at Gate 1, usually marked with a green or white sign that says "Sarv Darshan" or "General Darshan." Temple volunteers in orange or saffron vests will be present to guide you.

**Tips:** Join the general queue during the mid-morning lull (around 9:00 to 10:00 AM on weekdays). This is when most early risers have already completed their darshan and the afternoon crowd has not yet arrived. The line thins noticeably during this window.

**Special Darshan Queue (Paid)**

For ₹51 to ₹201, depending on the category selected, you get priority access through a separate counter near Gate 1. This counter is usually to the left of the main general queue entrance. The special darshan ticket includes a shorter wait and sometimes a brief individual interaction with the priests at the sanctum.

**How to identify it:** Look for a separate counter near Gate 1 with a sign for "Special Darshan" or "Yatra Darshan." The counter staff will explain the different categories and their prices. Payment can be made in cash.

**Tips:** If it is a festival day, arrive at the special darshan counter as early as 5:00 AM. The paid queue slots fill up quickly during peak seasons, and once they are gone, you are directed to the general queue. This is one of the best uses of a small amount of money during a crowded festival visit.

**Bhasma Aarti Queue (Advance Booking Required)**

This is the most structured queue. Once you have booked your slot online and received a confirmation, you arrive at the designated Bhasma Aarti entry gate — which may be a side entrance near Gate 1 rather than the main gate itself. Present your booking confirmation and original ID at the counter, receive your assigned seat number, and enter through the dedicated passage.

**How to identify it:** The Bhasma Aarti entry is clearly marked on festival days with banners and signage. Temple staff will specifically ask for your booking reference. Do not confuse this with the regular entry.

**Tips:** Keep both a digital copy and a printed copy of your booking confirmation on your person. Printouts are especially useful if your phone battery is low or if the network is poor at the venue. Arrive at least 90 minutes before the scheduled Bhasma Aarti time — there is security screening and seating assignment that takes time.

**Electronic Token System (Festival Days Only)**

During major festivals like Mahashivratri, Nag Panchami, and Maha Shivaratri, the temple management replaces the physical queue with an electronic token system. You register your name and mobile number at a kiosk or via the official app, receive a token number, and wait for your number to be called at a designated assembly point.

**How to identify it:** Look for large LED displays near the temple complex showing current token numbers. Volunteers will be stationed at the registration counters. Follow the announcements on the public address system.

**Tips:** Register for the token system as early as possible — ideally the evening before a major festival. Many devotees camp overnight near the temple during Mahashivratri specifically to get an early token number. If you are staying at Mahakal Bhakta Nivas, our front desk can help you check the current token status and guide you to the registration counters before dawn.

## Darshan by Season

Ujjain and Mahakaleshwar Temple feel very different depending on the season you visit. Each season brings its own atmosphere, crowd levels, and practical considerations.

**Shravan Season (July–August)**

Shravan is the holiest month in the Hindu calendar, dedicated to Lord Shiva. Millions of devotees visit Ujjain during this month, especially on Mondays (Shravan Somvar). The temple is vibrant, the queues are long, and the spiritual energy is extraordinary — but the heat is intense and crowds are overwhelming.

If you visit during Shravan:
- Expect wait times of 1.5 to 3 hours even in the general queue on Mondays.
- Book your darshan and accommodation well in advance. [Rooms near Mahakaleshwar Temple](/rooms/) fill up weeks ahead during peak Shravan weekends.
- Carry extra water and sun protection for the time you spend in the queue before entering the temple complex.
- Consider the paid special darshan option if your time in Ujjain is limited.
- The Bhasma Aarti during Shravan is especially powerful. Book online as soon as your travel dates are confirmed.

**Winter Season (November–February)**

This is the most comfortable season to visit Ujjain. Daytime temperatures range from 15 to 28 degrees Celsius, making the walk to the temple pleasant. Mornings can be cool — bring a shawl or light jacket for the pre-dawn darshan. Crowds are manageable, and the darshan experience is peaceful.

If you visit during winter:
- Morning darshan at 6:15 AM is magical — the cool air, the quiet corridors, and the golden morning light create an unforgettable atmosphere. For a detailed walkthrough of what to expect inside the temple, read our [inside Mahakaleshwar Temple walkthrough guide](/blog/inside-mahakaleshwar-temple-walkthrough/).
- Evening darshan with the Sandhya Aarti at 7:00 PM is equally beautiful. The cool evening air and the lit lamps make for a deeply moving experience. Check the [complete aarti timings list](/blog/mahakaleshwar-aarti-timings-list/) for all daily rituals.
- This is the best season for combining darshan with sightseeing around Ujjain — the Ram Ghat, Kal Bhairav Temple, and Harsiddhi Temple are all easily accessible.
- You can easily do both morning and evening darshans on the same day without exhaustion.

**Summer Season (April–June)**

Summer in Ujjain is harsh. Daytime temperatures regularly exceed 42 degrees Celsius, and the sun is relentless. Despite this, summer sees steady temple traffic, especially during the cooler early morning and late evening windows.

If you visit during summer:
- Stick to the 6:00–8:00 AM darshan window. The sun rises early and the queue area becomes unbearable by 10:00 AM.
- Carry more water than you think you need. The queue area has limited shade.
- Wear a cotton kurta or dhoti — natural fabrics breathe better in the heat.
- Avoid midday travel between your accommodation and the temple. The roads are hot and empty.
- Evening darshan after 5:00 PM is tolerable, but the Sandhya Aarti crowd in the summer heat can be overwhelming.

**Festival Seasons**

Ujjain's festival calendar is packed with major events. Mahashivratri (February/March) is the biggest — the entire city transforms into a massive open-air temple overnight. Diwali, Nag Panchami, and Kartik Purnima also draw huge crowds.

If you visit during a major festival:
- Book accommodation months in advance. Our [rooms at Mahakal Bhakta Nivas](/rooms/) are often fully booked 30 to 45 days before Mahashivratri.
- Check the official Ujjain Temple website and our front desk for the latest darshan timings and entry procedures — these change every year based on crowd management needs.
- Expect the full darshan experience to take 2 to 4 hours during peak festival hours.
- Arrive with a flexible mindset. Festival darshan is a communal, overwhelming, beautiful experience — it is not a quick visit.
- If the crowds feel too much, step into the Mahakal Lok Corridor for a quieter moment of reflection.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

First-time visitors often make avoidable mistakes that turn a spiritually uplifting experience into a frustrating one. Here are the most common pitfalls and how to steer clear of them:

1. **Arriving without knowing the queue system.** Walk up to Gate 1 and you will see three or four different lines. If you do not know which one to join, you will waste time standing in the wrong queue. Always check with a temple volunteer or look for the overhead signage before joining any line. The general darshan line is usually the longest and most clearly marked.

2. **Carrying valuables into the queue.** This is the single most common regret. Visitors carry phones, wallets, and cameras into the queue, then panic when asked to deposit them at the cloakroom at the last moment. Pack light from the start — leave everything except your ID and a small cash pouch at your accommodation. Mahakal Bhakta Nivas guests have the advantage of secure locker storage right at the property.

3. **Dressing inappropriately and getting turned away at the gate.** Nothing delays your darshan more than being stopped at the entry because of your clothes. Shorts, sleeveless tops, and short dresses are not allowed. If you are unsure, dress more conservatively than you think you need to. A simple full-length pant and a collared shirt for men, and a long kurta or salwar kameez for women, always works.

4. **Skipping the cloakroom and trying to hide your phone.** Some visitors try to keep their phones in their pockets or small pouches. The security check at the sanctum entrance is thorough, and having your phone confiscated mid-queue is far more disruptive than using the cloakroom. The nominal locker fee is worth every rupee for the peace of mind.

5. **Rushing through the darshan.** Many visitors treat darshan like a checklist item — join the queue, get inside, take a quick look at the lingam, and leave. The sanctum moment itself is brief, but the entire experience — the chants, the incense, the faces of fellow devotees, the weight of centuries of devotion in the corridors — is what makes darshan meaningful. Slow down and be present.

6. **Not planning for the return trip.** Visitors often focus entirely on reaching the temple and forget that they need to get back. After a long darshan, especially during peak hours, the exit routes can be congested. Local transport may be unavailable or slow. If you are staying within walking distance at Mahakal Bhakta Nivas, you simply walk back — no waiting for auto-rickshaws or taxis in a crowd.

7. **Forgetting ID for Bhasma Aarti.** This is non-negotiable. The temple authorities require original government-issued photo ID at the Bhasma Aarti entry. A photocopy or a digital copy on your phone is not accepted at many checkpoints. Keep your Aadhaar card, voter ID, or passport in a safe but accessible place throughout the visit.

8. **Visiting during Mahashivratri without a plan.** Mahashivratri is not a normal darshan day. The crowds, the waiting times, and the energy levels are unlike anything else. Many first-time visitors show up without any preparation and spend the entire night in a crowded queue with no food, water, or rest. Plan ahead: arrange your accommodation, bring snacks and water, and know the entry procedures before you arrive.

9. **Not verifying timings before you go.** Darshan hours, aarti schedules, and entry procedures change periodically, especially around festivals. Always check the official Ujjain Mahakaleshwar Temple website or call our front desk at Mahakal Bhakta Nivas for the latest timings before heading out. Relying on an outdated blog post or a friend's memory from a previous visit can lead to missed darshans and unnecessary confusion.

10. **Eating heavy meals before darshan.** A full, heavy meal before joining a long queue in a crowded temple environment is uncomfortable and can lead to indigestion or nausea, especially in the early morning heat. Eat light before darshan — a piece of fruit, some curd, or plain bread. Save your big meal for after the darshan when you are back at your accommodation and relaxed.

You can still enter the temple complex, walk through the outer halls, witness the aarti, and experience the atmosphere. Here is a strategy that works: arrive by 5:00 AM, join the general queue (or buy a special darshan ticket if you want a shorter wait), and be prepared for a 1.5 to 2 hour experience. Wear comfortable shoes for the wait, carry a water bottle, and have a light breakfast before joining the queue. Alternatively, consider visiting on a Tuesday or Wednesday during Shravan, when the crowds are noticeably thinner. For a more relaxed experience overall, visit in winter rather than Shravan. The temple's official booking portal sends an email notification with the refund details. Keep your booking confirmation and transaction reference safe. If you do not receive a refund within the stated timeframe, contact the temple's help desk or the official Ujjain Temple helpline. Our front desk at Mahakal Bhakta Nivas can assist guests with follow-up on refund matters.

**Q: What time does the temple actually close, and can I do darshan right before closing?**

A: Mahakaleshwar Temple officially closes at 11:00 PM. The last entry for general darshan is usually around 10:30 PM, but this varies during festivals when extended hours apply. Nighttime darshan (Shayan Aarti) is a beautiful, quieter experience if you can make it before closing. The Shayan Aarti happens around 10:00 PM and is less crowded than the evening Sandhya Aarti. However, do not plan a darshan too close to the closing time — the temple staff begins clearing the complex 15 to 20 minutes before the official closing hour, and you may be turned away. Always arrive with at least 45 minutes of buffer time if you are visiting in the late evening.

## Special Darshan Experiences in Depth

Beyond the standard darshan, Mahakaleshwar Temple offers several special rituals and experiences that can transform your visit from a routine darshan into a deeply personal spiritual moment.

**Bhasma Aarti — The Sacred Ash Ritual**

The Bhasma Aarti is Mahakaleshwar's most famous ritual. Performed every day at 4:00 AM before the main temple opens, this ceremony involves the priests anointing the Jyotirlinga with sacred ash (bhasma) while chanting the Rudram and other Vedic hymns. The atmosphere during Bhasma Aarti is unlike anything else — the dim temple lights, the rhythmic chanting, the fragrance of incense and ghee lamps, and the quiet devotion of a small, seated audience create an experience that stays with you for a lifetime.

Booking is mandatory and is done online through the official Ujjain Temple portal. Slots open up a few weeks in advance and are snapped up quickly, especially during festival seasons. The process requires you to submit your name, photo ID number, and a digital photo at the time of booking. On the day of the Aarti, arrive with your booking confirmation (printout preferred) and the original ID you used for booking. For a complete step-by-step guide to the booking process, see our [Bhasma Aarti booking guide](/blog/how-to-book-bhasma-aarti-mahakaleshwar/).

**Rudrabhishek — The Sacred Bathing Ritual**

Rudrabhishek is one of the most powerful and ancient rituals dedicated to Lord Shiva. During this abhishek, the Shiva lingam is bathed with a sequence of sacred substances: milk (representing purity), curd (representing prosperity), honey (representing sweetness and longevity), ghee (representing light and knowledge), and sacred ash (representing detachment). Each pour is accompanied by the chanting of the Rudram from the Krishna Yajurveda.

Rudrabhishek at Mahakaleshwar can be arranged through the temple counter on the day of your visit. The donation amount varies based on the scale of the ritual — ask at the seva counter near the main hall. The ritual takes approximately 30 to 45 minutes and is performed by the temple priests on your behalf. You do not need to be present for the entire ritual, though many devotees choose to stay and witness the abhishek from the designated area.

**Laghu Rudrabhishek — A Shorter Alternative**

If the full Rudrabhishek is beyond your budget or time constraints, the Laghu Rudrabhishek (meaning "small" or "short" Rudrabhishek) is a wonderful alternative. This condensed version includes the essential elements of the ritual in a shorter timeframe, typically 15 to 20 minutes. It is suitable for individual devotees and can be arranged on any regular day without advance booking. Visit the temple seva counter upon arrival and ask for the Laghu Rudrabhishek option.

**Maha Rudrabhishek — The Grand Ceremony**

On special occasions and during major festivals, the temple organizes a Maha Rudrabhishek — a grand, elaborate version of the ritual. This involves 11 or 16 rounds of abhishek (depending on the occasion), the participation of multiple priests, and a larger donation contribution. The Maha Rudrabhishek is typically organized by the temple management rather than by individual devotees, and you can participate by contributing to the collective donation. Inquire at the temple office for upcoming Maha Rudrabhishek dates.

**Panchamrit Abhishek**

Panchamrit — the five nectars of milk, curd, honey, ghee, and sugar — is poured over the Shiva lingam during this special abhishek. It is considered one of the most purifying rituals in Hinduism. Panchamrit Abhishek at Mahakaleshwar can be booked at the temple counter. Many devotees perform this abhishek on their own birthdays, anniversaries, or during significant life events. The prasad (holy offering) distributed after the Panchamrit Abhishek is considered especially auspicious.

**Pind Daan and Tarpan Rituals**

For devotees wishing to perform pind daan (ritual offerings for departed ancestors) or tarpan (water oblation for ancestors), Mahakaleshwar Temple provides dedicated facilities. The priests guide you through the ritual steps, which typically take 20 to 30 minutes. These rituals are especially meaningful when performed at a Jyotirlinga, as it is believed that offerings made here reach the ancestors directly. Inquire at the temple office for the current procedures, timings, and donation amounts.

**Akhand Ramayan Path**

On certain auspicious days, the temple organizes an Akhand Ramayan Path — a continuous, uninterrupted recitation of the Ramayana that runs for 24 hours. Devotees can sit and listen for as long as they wish, and many participate by reciting verses themselves. The atmosphere during the Ramayan Path is deeply devotional and meditative. Check the temple notice board or our front desk for the schedule.

## What Happens After Darshan

The darshan experience does not end when you exit the sanctum. What happens after darshan — the prasad, the exit, the quiet moment of reflection — is an important part of the overall experience.

**Collecting Your Prasad**

After darshan, you will receive prasad at the exit counter near the sanctum. This typically includes a small packet of dry fruit, a piece of sweet peda, and sometimes a tulsi leaf or rudraksha bead, depending on the day and festival season. If you have sponsored a special abhishek or sewa, you may receive additional prasad items, including a packaged prasad box that you can carry home.

Near the exit, there are also vendors selling fresh garlands, coconuts, and small brass or copper items that you can take home as mementos of your darshan. Be aware that some of these items may be overpriced within the temple complex — the same items are available at lower prices in the markets outside the temple gates.

**Exit Routes**

After collecting your prasad, you can exit through the same Gate 1 (Singh Dwar) you entered from, or you can take the alternative exit near the Mahakal Lok corridor if you want to extend your walk. The exit near the Mahakal Lok corridor is less crowded and leads you through a quieter section of the temple market area, which is pleasant for a slow walk after the intensity of the darshan.

**What to Do Next**

Many visitors find that the first 10 to 15 minutes after darshan are a quiet, contemplative period. You are still carrying the energy of the sanctum, and the world outside has not yet rushed back in. Use this time:

- Walk slowly toward the exit rather than rushing. The corridors of the temple have their own atmosphere.
- If you are hungry, stop at one of the clean vegetarian restaurants near the temple gate. A simple thali of dal, roti, and rice is restorative after a long morning.
- Head back to your accommodation for a rest. If you are at Mahakal Bhakta Nivas, our rooms are a 10-minute walk away, and you can return to a comfortable, quiet space to reflect on your experience.
- If you plan a second darshan in the evening, use the afternoon to rest, eat lightly, and recharge. A midday nap after morning darshan is a ritual many of our guests swear by.

**Returning for Evening Darshan**

If you want to experience the Sandhya Aarti in the evening, plan your day accordingly:
- Rest after the morning darshan and have a light lunch.
- Return to the temple by 6:00 PM for the evening entry.
- The evening crowd builds up quickly — by 6:30 PM the general queue is already substantial.
- Position yourself near the front of the queue for the best view of the aarti.
- After the evening darshan, exit through the Mahakal Lok corridor for a night walk under the illuminated sculptures.

For detailed information about all aarti timings throughout the day, including the morning, afternoon, and evening rituals, see our [complete Mahakaleshwar aarti timings list](/blog/mahakaleshwar-aarti-timings-list/).

## Darshan Do's and Don'ts

Here is a quick-reference table of what to do and what to avoid during your Mahakaleshwar darshan:

| Do's | Don'ts |
|---|---|
| Arrive at least 30 minutes before your planned darshan time | Do not arrive late for Bhasma Aarti — late arrivals are not admitted |
| Dress in modest, traditional clothing covering shoulders and knees | Do not wear shorts, sleeveless tops, or revealing clothing |
| Deposit all electronics at the cloakroom before joining the queue | Do not try to hide your phone or camera — security checks are thorough |
| Carry a small cash pouch for prasad and cloakroom fees | Do not bring large bags, backpacks, or expensive items into the queue |
| Join the correct queue line after checking with temple staff | Do not push, shove, or jump the queue — this is a place of worship |
| Maintain silence and reverence inside the temple corridors | Do not take photos or videos inside the temple — it is strictly prohibited |
| Remove your shoes before entering any temple hall | Do not wear shoes or socks inside the temple complex |
| Verify darshan timings on the official portal before visiting | Do not rely on outdated information from old travel guides |
| Keep your original ID with you if you have a Bhasma Aarti booking | Do not bring leather items (belts, wallets, watch straps) inside |
| Walk calmly and mindfully through the temple corridors | Do not rush through the darshan — the experience is as much about the journey as the destination |
| Ask temple staff for directions if you are unsure about the layout | Do not wander into restricted areas — respect all barricades and signage |
| Offer a small donation if you feel moved to do so | Do not bargain or argue with temple staff or priests |
