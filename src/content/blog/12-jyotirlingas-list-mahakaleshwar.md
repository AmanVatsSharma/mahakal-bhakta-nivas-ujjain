# 12 Jyotirlingas of India: Complete List, Map & Mahakaleshwar's Place Among Them

The **Jyotirlingas** are the twelve most sacred shrines of Lord Shiva in Hinduism. Each Jyotirlinga represents a spot where Shiva appeared as a fiery column of light — a divine self-manifestation that no human could fully comprehend. Visiting all twelve Jyotirlingas is considered one of the most sacred pilgrimages a Hindu devotee can undertake.

At the heart of this pilgrimage is **Mahakaleshwar in Ujjain** — the only south-facing Jyotirlinga, home of the legendary Bhasma Aarti, and one of the most spiritually powerful temples in India.

This post lists all 12 Jyotirlingas with their locations, unique features, and a suggested circuit for devotees who want to complete the full pilgrimage — starting from Ujjain.

## The Complete List of 12 Jyotirlingas

### 1. Somnath — Gujarat

**Location:** Prabhas Patan, Gujarat (west coast)
**Significance:** The first Jyotirlinga, Somnath is mentioned in the Rigveda. It has been destroyed and rebuilt multiple times — most famously by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1026 CE. The current temple was reconstructed in 1951.
**Unique feature:** Located on the Arabian Sea coast. The sound of waves is said to be Shiva's own mantra.
**Best time to visit:** October–March

### 2. Mallikarjuna — Andhra Pradesh

**Location:** Srisailam, Andhra Pradesh (Nallamala Hills)
**Significance:** One of the oldest Jyotirlingas, mentioned in the Mahabharata. It is also a Shakti Peetha (abode of Goddess Parvati/Bramarambika), making it a powerful dual shrine.
**Unique feature:** Set in the dense Nallamala forest, the temple has a remote, spiritual atmosphere.
**Best time to visit:** October–February

### 3. Mahakaleshwar — Madhya Pradesh

**Location:** Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh
**Significance:** One of the 12 Jyotirlingas, one of the 7 Mokshapuri, one of the 4 Kumbh Mela cities, and the site of the legendary Bhasma Aarti.
**Unique feature:** The only **south-facing** Jyotirlinga. The Bhasma Aarti uses sacred ash (bhasma) as the offering — a ritual unique to this temple.
**Best time to visit:** October–March; Shivaratri for the ultimate experience

### 4. Omkareshwar — Madhya Pradesh

**Location:** Omkareshwar, Madhya Pradesh (Narmada River)
**Significance:** The Jyotirlinga of Lord Shiva as Omkareshwar (the Lord of the Om syllable). The island temple is shaped like the sacred symbol Om.
**Unique feature:** The temple sits on an island in the Narmada River — you can walk around the Om-shaped island (parikrama).
**Best time to visit:** October–March

### 5. Kedarnath — Uttarakhand

**Location:** Kedarnath, Uttarakhand (Himalayas)
**Significance:** The northernmost Jyotirlinga, associated with the Pandavas from the Mahabharata. The temple is at 3,583 meters altitude and accessible only by trek.
**Unique feature:** The highest and most demanding Jyotirlinga. The temple is open only from April to November due to snow.
**Best time to visit:** May–June, September–October

### 6. Bhimashankar — Maharashtra

**Location:** Bhimashankar, Maharashtra (Sahyadri Hills)
**Significance:** The Jyotirlinga associated with the demon Bhima and Lord Shiva's victory over him. It is also a wildlife sanctuary.
**Unique feature:** The temple is within a dense forest and near a beautiful waterfall. A nature pilgrimage combined with a spiritual one.
**Best time to visit:** October–February

### 7. Kashi Vishwanath — Uttar Pradesh

**Location:** Varanasi (Kashi), Uttar Pradesh
**Significance:** Perhaps the most famous Jyotirlinga, located in the holiest city for Hindus. The current temple was rebuilt by Maharani Ahilyabai Holkar.
**Unique feature:** In Varanasi — the city of death and liberation. The Ganga aarti at the adjacent ghats is world-famous.
**Best time to visit:** October–March

### 8. Trimbakeshwar — Maharashtra

**Location:** Trimbakeshwar, near Nashik, Maharashtra
**Significance:** The Jyotirlinga associated with the origin of the Godavari River. It is also one of the 12 Aaditya (solar) shrines.
**Unique feature:** The Jyotirlinga itself is a three-faced lingam representing Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva. The Godavari originates from this region.
**Best time to visit:** October–March

### 9. Vaidyanath — Jharkhand

**Location:** Deoghar, Jharkhand
**Significance:** The Jyotirlinga of Vaidyanath (Lord of Physicians). Shiva appeared here as a doctor to heal his devotee.
**Unique feature:** Associated with the legendary Shravani Mela — a 21-day pilgrimage where devotees carry holy Ganga water from Sultanganj (105 km) to offer to the Jyotirlinga.
**Best time to visit:** July–August (Shravani Mela); October–March

### 10. Nageshwar — Gujarat

**Location:** Dwarka, Gujarat
**Significance:** The Jyotirlinga associated with Lord Shiva's protection of his devotees from all directions (Nagesh = Lord of Nagas/directions). Near Krishna's Dwarka.
**Unique feature:** Close to the Dwarkadheesh Temple (Krishna). A combined Krishna-Shiva pilgrimage is possible.
**Best time to visit:** October–March

### 11. Rameshwaram — Tamil Nadu

**Location:** Rameshwaram, Tamil Nadu
**Significance:** The Jyotirlinga installed by Lord Rama before his journey to Lanka. One of the Char Dham.
**Unique feature:** The temple has the longest corridor of any Hindu temple in India (over 1,000 meters of pillared halls). The Ramanathaswamy Temple is a masterpiece of Dravidian architecture.
**Best time to visit:** October–March

### 12. Grishneshwar — Maharashtra

**Location:** Near Ellora Caves, Aurangabad, Maharashtra
**Significance:** The smallest of the 12 Jyotirlingas, also called Ghushmeshwar. It is near the UNESCO-listed Ellora Caves.
**Unique feature:** Located near Ellora — you can combine a visit to the Kailasa Temple (Ellora Caves) with the Jyotirlinga darshan.
**Best time to visit:** October–March

## Mahakaleshwar's Unique Place Among the 12

Mahakaleshwar stands out among the 12 Jyotirlingas for several reasons:

**1. South-Facing Orientation**
Every other Jyotirlinga faces east. Mahakaleshwar faces south — a direction associated with Yama, the god of death, and with the concept of time itself. This orientation reflects Shiva's role as Mahakal — the lord of time and death.

**2. The Bhasma Aarti**
No other Jyotirlinga has the Bhasma Aarti — a ritual where the Shiva lingam is bathed with sacred ash (bhasma) collected from homa fires, accompanied by elaborate mantras. This aarti begins at 4 AM and requires special passes. It is the most sought-after ritual at Mahakaleshwar and draws pilgrims from across the world.

**3. One of the Four Kumbh Mela Cities**
Ujjain is one of only four cities in India that hosts the Kumbh Mela (Simhastha). This status places it in a league with Prayagraj, Haridwar, and Nashik — cities of extraordinary spiritual importance.

**4. Continuously Active for 2,000+ Years**
While several Jyotirlingas have been destroyed and rebuilt, Mahakaleshwar's temple has been in continuous worship since ancient times. The current structure was built by Maharani Ahilyabai Holkar in the 18th century, but the worship of the Jyotirlinga predates it by millennia.

## Suggested Jyotirlinga Circuits

### Circuit 1: The MP Circuit (Easiest, Best for First-Timers)

This is the most accessible Jyotirlinga circuit, covering two Jyotirlingas in Madhya Pradesh within a 300 km radius.

**Route:** Ujjain (Mahakaleshwar) → Omkareshwar → Ujjain
**Duration:** 3–5 days
**Best time:** October–March

Day 1: Arrive Ujjain, check in, evening darshan at Mahakaleshwar
Day 2: Bhasma Aarti at Mahakaleshwar, morning aarti, afternoon rest, evening darshan
Day 3: Travel to Omkareshwar (2.5–3 hours from Ujjain), darshan at Omkareshwar, Om-shaped island parikrama
Day 4: Return to Ujjain, final darshan, departure

### Circuit 2: The Western India Circuit (Extended)

Covers three Jyotirlingas in western India for devotees who want a deeper experience.

**Route:** Somnath (Gujarat) → Dwarka/Nageshwar (Gujarat) → Ujjain (Madhya Pradesh)
**Duration:** 7–10 days

### Circuit 3: The Grand 12-Jyotirlinga Circuit

For serious pilgrims, visiting all 12 Jyotirlingas is a multi-year journey. Here is a suggested route organized geographically:

**South India first:**
Rameshwaram (Tamil Nadu) → Trimbakeshwar (Maharashtra) → Bhimashankar (Maharashtra)

**Central India:**
Grishneshwar (Maharashtra) → Omkareshwar (MP) → Mahakaleshwar (Ujjain, MP)

**North India:**
Vaidyanath (Jharkhand) → Baidyanath Dham → Kashi Vishwanath (UP) → Kedarnath (Uttarakhand)

**West India:**
Nageshwar (Gujarat) → Somnath (Gujarat) → Mallikarjuna (Andhra Pradesh — can be combined with a South tour)

**Duration:** 3–4 months if done continuously, or split across 3–4 years by region
**Budget:** ₹1,00,000–₹2,00,000 per person for the full circuit

### Practical Tips for Jyotirlinga Pilgrimages

1. **Start from Ujjain.** It is well-connected by rail and road from Indore, and the Mahakaleshwar experience sets the spiritual tone for the entire journey.
2. **Book accommodation in advance** for each Jyotirlinga. Kedarnath, for instance, has extremely limited options.
3. **Carry religious identification** (passport/Aadhaar) — some temples require it for special darshan passes.
4. **Check seasonal accessibility.** Kedarnath is closed in winter. Some temples have restricted entry during certain festivals.
5. **Start with the MP Circuit.** Mahakaleshwar and Omkareshwar are the easiest Jyotirlingas to visit. Use this to build your pilgrimage stamina before tackling harder destinations like Kedarnath or Mallikarjuna.

## FAQs

**Q1: Can I visit all 12 Jyotirlingas in one trip?**
It is logistically possible but extremely demanding. Most devotees spread the 12 Jyotirlingas across 2–3 years. Start with the MP circuit (Mahakaleshwar + Omkareshwar) and gradually expand.

**Q2: Is there a specific order in which to visit the Jyotirlingas?**
There is no fixed order, but many devotees start from Somnath (the first Jyotirlinga) or from Rameshwaram (Rama's Jyotirlinga). Starting from Ujjain (Mahakaleshwar) is also popular.

**Q3: What is special about Mahakaleshwar compared to other Jyotirlingas?**
Mahakaleshwar is the only south-facing Jyotirlinga and the only one with the Bhasma Aarti. It is also one of the 7 Mokshapuri, making it one of the most spiritually potent cities in Hinduism.

**Q4: How many days are needed for the MP Jyotirlinga circuit?**
3–5 days cover both Mahakaleshwar (Ujjain) and Omkareshwar comfortably. 2 days in Ujjain and 1–2 days in Omkareshwar.
