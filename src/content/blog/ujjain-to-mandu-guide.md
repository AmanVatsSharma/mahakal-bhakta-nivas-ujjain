Pilgrims know Ujjain as the city of **Mahakaleshwar**, one of the 12 most sacred Jyotirlingas
of Lord Shiva. But for those extending their spiritual journey through Madhya Pradesh, just
150 km to the south lies another Jyotirlinga of equal significance — **Omkareshwar**, one of
the 12 holiest abodes of Lord Shiva, sitting on the banks of the **Narmada River** on a
beautiful island shaped like the *Om* symbol itself.

This guide covers every transport option from Ujjain to Omkareshwar, practical tips, and
how to fit this pilgrimage into your broader Madhya Pradesh spiritual itinerary.

## Distance and Journey Overview

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Distance (by road) | 140–150 km |
| Typical journey time | 3.5–4.5 hours |
| Main route | Ujjain → Khandwa → Omkareshwar |
| Alternate route | Ujjain → Indore → Khandwa → Omkareshwar |
| Best time to travel | Oct–Mar (avoid peak summer) |

The most direct route goes via **Khandwa** on the Ujjain–Khandwa highway (MP State Highway
27). The road is generally in good condition, though some stretches near the Narmada
valley can be narrow and winding. Traffic is moderate — the highway connects Ujjain to
the broader Indore–Khandwa–Nagpur corridor.

## Transport Options from Ujjain

### Taxi / Private Car (Most Recommended)

The most convenient and flexible option for a Jyotirlinga visit is a hired car with a driver.

| Type | One-Way Cost | Round Trip | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hatchback (Swift, Dzire) | ₹2,500–3,000 | ₹4,000–5,000 | Good for 4 people |
| SUV (Innova, XUV) | ₹3,500–4,500 | ₹5,500–7,000 | Better for groups |
| Shared taxi | ₹400–600 per seat | — | Less flexible for timings |

A hired car gives you full control over darshan timings, stops at the **Narmada ghats**,
and the freedom to visit both the Omkareshwar and **Mamaleshwar** temples on the island.
Book from a Ujjain travel agent or ask your hotel — most hotels including
[Mahakal Bhakta Nivas](/rooms/) can arrange taxis on request.

### Bus (MP State Transport)

MP State Transport Corporation operates direct and connecting buses between Ujjain and
Omkareshwar.

- **Departure point:** Nana Kheda Bus Stand, Ujjain
- **Frequency:** 3–5 buses daily (fewer on Sundays)
- **Duration:** 4.5–5.5 hours (including stop at Khandwa)
- **Fare:** ₹200–350 per person (ordinary); ₹300–450 (Volvo/Mini)
- **Advance booking:** Available on the MP Tourism portal or at the bus stand

The bus drops you at the Omkareshwar bus stand, about 2 km from the temple. From there,
local autos (₹20–30) or a short walk gets you to the ghats.

**Tip:** The bus is economical for solo travellers and budget groups. However, it runs
on a fixed schedule, which can clash with temple darshan times. If you are on a tight
spiritual itinerary, a taxi is worth the extra cost.

### Train (via Khandwa)

There is no direct railway station at Omkareshwar. The nearest railway station is
**Khandwa Junction (KNW)**, approximately 55 km from Omkareshwar. From Khandwa, you
can take a taxi or local bus to Omkareshwar.

**From Ujjain to Khandwa by train:**

- Multiple daily trains connect Ujjain Junction (UJN) to Khandwa Junction (KNW)
- Journey time: approximately 3.5–5 hours depending on the train
- Trains include express and passenger services
- Ticket booking via IRCTC

From Khandwa, a taxi to Omkareshwar costs approximately ₹1,000–1,500 and takes about
1.5–2 hours. The road from Khandwa to Omkareshwar is scenic but narrow in parts.

**Combined train + taxi approach:** Best if you want to experience the Indian Railways
and have flexible temple timings. Book the train in advance during peak pilgrimage seasons
(Mahashivratri, Sawan).

## Omkareshwar Temple Details

### About the Jyotirlinga

**Omkareshwar** is one of the 12 Jyotirlingas of Lord Shiva. The name comes from the
fact that the island on which the main temple sits is shaped like the sacred **Om**
symbol. The temple sits on an island in the Narmada River, connected to the mainland
by a bridge and two ghats.

There are actually two main temples on the island:

1. **Omkareshwar Temple** (on the island) — The primary Jyotirlinga shrine
2. **Mamaleshwar Temple** (on the mainland) — Considered the guardian temple; both
   are visited together as part of the full darshan circuit

### Temple Timings

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 5:00 AM | Temple opens |
| 5:30–7:00 AM | Mangala Aarti (most peaceful darshan) |
| 11:00 AM–12:30 PM | Darshan window |
| 4:30–7:00 PM | Evening darshan |
| 7:30–8:00 PM | Evening Aarti on Narmada ghats |
| 10:00 PM | Temple closes |

### Darshan Tips

- **Early morning is best** — arrive by 5:30 AM for a relatively peaceful experience.
  The Mangala Aarti at dawn, with the Narmada flowing beside the temple, is deeply moving.
- **Monday (Sawan)** — expect very large crowds. Arrive by 4:30 AM or consider
  overnighting in Omkareshwar town.
- **Mahashivratri** — the temple and the entire town come alive. Special arrangements
  are made. Book accommodation weeks in advance.
- **Abhishek participation** — there is usually a separate queue for abhishek
  (ritual bathing of the Shiva Linga). Carry cash for offerings.
- **Photography** — not permitted inside the main temple sanctum. Photography is
  allowed in the outer courtyard and ghats.

## How to Combine Omkareshwar with Your Ujjain Trip

The Ujjain–Omkareshwar combination is a powerful two-Jyotirlinga pilgrimage that many
devotees complete in 2–3 days. Here is a practical itinerary:

### 3-Day Ujjain + Omkareshwar Itinerary

**Day 1: Ujjain — Mahakaleshwar Darshan**

- Arrive in Ujjain (by train, bus, or flight to Indore)
- Check in near Mahakaleshwar — [our rooms at Mahakal Bhakta Nivas](/rooms/) are
  just 200 m from the temple gate
- Evening darshan and Sandhya Aarti at Mahakaleshwar
- Dinner at a pure-veg restaurant near the temple

**Day 2: Ujjain to Omkareshwar**

- Wake up early, Bhasma Aarti at Mahakaleshwar (4 AM booking required)
- Depart Ujjain by 6:30–7:00 AM by hired taxi (recommended) or bus
- Reach Omkareshwar by 10:00–10:30 AM
- Darshan at Omkareshwar Temple and Mamaleshwar Temple
- Lunch at a restaurant on the Narmada ghats
- Explore the island, walk the ghats, visit local temples
- Evening aarti at the Narmada ghats
- Overnight at Omkareshwar or return to Ujjain (late departure, ~9 PM arrival)

**Day 3: Return to Ujjain or Continue**

- Return to Ujjain by morning taxi or afternoon bus
- Visit Kal Bhairav Temple or other local sites
- Proceed to Indore, Maheshwar, or onward journey

### Extended: Add Maheshwar to the Loop

For a more relaxed 4-day pilgrimage:

- **Day 1:** Ujjain (Mahakaleshwar)
- **Day 2:** Ujjain → Maheshwar (50 km) — Narmada ghats, Ahilya Fort, handloom sarees
- **Day 3:** Maheshwar → Omkareshwar (80 km) — Omkareshwar darshan
- **Day 4:** Omkareshwar → Ujjain → onward

## Practical Tips for Omkareshwar

1. **Carry cash** — small shops and local guides on the island prefer cash. Network
   coverage can be patchy near the river.
2. **Dress modestly** — both temples require covered shoulders and legs. Carry a scarf
   or shawl.
3. **Walking on the island** — the Omkareshwar island circuit (visiting both temples)
   involves walking about 2–3 km. Wear comfortable shoes.
4. **Boat ride on Narmada** — traditional wooden boats are available on the Narmada
   near the ghats (₹100–200 per person). A peaceful way to experience the river, especially
   at sunrise or sunset.
5. **Accommodation** — Omkareshwar town has budget hotels and dharamshalas. For a more
   comfortable base, return to Ujjain and stay at [Mahakal Bhakta Nivas](/rooms/)
   with rooms from ₹1,250.
6. **Food** — the island has simple vegetarian food stalls. For a proper meal, eat at
   restaurants on the mainland near the bridge.
7. **Weekday vs weekend** — Omkareshwar is quieter on weekdays. Plan your visit
   accordingly if you prefer a peaceful darshan.
8. **Combined with Indore** — if you are flying out of Indore, Omkareshwar makes a
   convenient last stop before the airport (about 70 km from Indore, 1.5 hours).

## FAQ

**Q: Is Omkareshwar better than Mahakaleshwar?**
A: Both are Jyotirlingas of immense spiritual significance. Mahakaleshwar in Ujjain is
one of the most powerful and is deeply integrated into the city's spiritual fabric.
Omkareshwar offers a quieter, more riverside experience on a beautiful island. Many
pilgrims visit both as complementary darshans.

**Q: Can I visit both Jyotirlingas in one day?**
A: Not realistically — they are 150 km apart. Plan at least 2 days: one for Ujjain
and one for Omkareshwar.

**Q: Are there accommodation options near Omkareshwar?**
A: Yes, there are budget hotels, dharamshalas, and MP Tourism rest houses. However,
options are limited during peak seasons. Many pilgrims prefer to base themselves in Ujjain
and do a day trip or overnight.

**Q: Is the Narmada safe for a dip?**
A: Yes, the Narmada is considered one of India's most sacred rivers, and a holy dip
is an integral part of the Omkareshwar pilgrimage. The ghats are well maintained, and
the water is generally clean near the temple area. Take normal river-bathing precautions.

**Q: What other temples are near Omkareshwar?**
A: The Mamaleshwar Temple (on the mainland), Siddnath Temple, and the Kajal Rani
Palace ruins are all within a short distance. The Govindraj Ji Temple on the banks of
the Narmada is also worth visiting.
