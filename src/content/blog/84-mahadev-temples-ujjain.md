Ask around the old city and you will hear the number before you hear the names: **84**. The **84 Mahadev mandir circuit of Ujjain** is one of the most beloved traditions of this temple town — the belief, old and firm, that the city shelters **84 sacred shrines of Shiva**, and that visiting them in the traditional order is a parikrama of rare merit. Pilgrims speak of it the way mountaineers speak of a classic route: not a sightseeing list but a full orbit of the divine. This guide explains the tradition, how the **84 Shiv mandir parikrama of Ujjain** actually works, and how to plan it — whether you give it one long day or two.

## The Tradition of 84

Eighty-four recurs across Indian sacred lore — in the counts of yogic asanas, of siddhas, of shrines in temple towns across the land — and tradition holds that Ujjain, as the city of Mahakal, gathers **84 Mahadev shrines** within its circle. The number is not an inventory to be audited; it is a way of saying that the city itself is a mandala of Shiva, with **Mahakaleshwar at its head** and every lane-corner linga counting as a part of the whole.

One honest note: ask three priests for the written list and you may see small variations in the minor stops. The great shrines are common to every version; the delight is in the small ones.

## How the Parikrama Works

The circuit is done in a **set traditional order**, beginning at Mahakaleshwar and threading outward through the old city — past Har Phatak, through the lanes behind the ghats, out along the Shipra, and back to close where it began. Devotees walk it, drive it, or most commonly ride it by auto, stopping at each shrine for a brief darshan, a flower, a touch of the rail and a moment of stillness.

Three honest ways to do it:

1. **The classic single day** — start before dawn, keep each stop short, finish by evening.
2. **The two-day split** — old city shrines on day one, outer and riverside shrines on day two; kinder to elders.
3. **The guided auto circuit** — a driver who knows the traditional order saves hours of navigation and missed lanes.

## Shrines You Will Remember

Every pilgrim returns with a private shortlist. The anchors are fixed: **Mahakaleshwar**, head of the circle, where the parikrama takes its first and last step; **Omkareshwar**, honoured within the Mahakal complex by tradition; **Nagchandreshwar** above it, if your day happens to be Nag Panchami. After the great shrines, the route fans into the lanes — and this is where the yatra earns its charm. You will duck into courtyard shrines where a priest unlocks a small door as though you were expected, find a linga under an old peepal with a brass bell gone green with age, and stop at riverside shrines where the Shipra is close enough to hear.

The lesson of the 84 arrives quietly somewhere in the middle: the circuit does not rank the shrines. The great temple with its queues and the lane-corner linga with its single diya count the same in the list, and walking them as equals is half the point.

## Mondays and Shravan: When the Circuit Glows

**Monday is the day of Shiva**, so a Monday parikrama is considered especially fruitful — and the old city shows it, with busier shrines and fresher flowers along the whole route. In **Shravan**, the monsoon month of Shiva, the circuit reaches its annual peak; our [Shravan Somvar guide](/blog/shravan-somvar-mahakaleshwar-guide/) covers what to expect when the entire city turns devotional. If you would rather have the lanes to yourself, an ordinary weekday morning is quiet and lovely.

## Planning the Yatra: A Sample Split

| Itinerary | Start time | Coverage | Suited for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One-day classic | Before dawn | All shrines, brief stops | Fit walkers and riders |
| Two-day relaxed | 6–7 AM each day | Old city day one, outer shrines day two | Families with elders |
| Auto circuit | After morning aarti | Full loop with a driver | First-time visitors |

A few things make the day kinder:

- **Start at Mahakal after an early darshan**, then begin the circuit so the closing stop feels like a homecoming.
- **Wear sandals you can slip off**, because shrine after shrine will ask for bare feet.
- **Carry small change** for offerings and for the lane priests who open doors for you.
- **Keep water and light snacks** in your bag, especially in the warmer months.
- **Ask for the traditional list** — old-city shops and priests often keep printed versions of the route order.

## Where to Stay for the Circuit

Because the circuit begins and ends at Mahakal, the smartest base is the old city centre near the temple. **Mahakal Bhakta Nivas on Gopal Mandir Road is about 200 m — a two to three minute walk — from the temple gate**, with pure veg food, attached bathrooms, hot water, WiFi, parking, power backup and [rooms from ₹1,250](/rooms/). You can step out at first light and start your parikrama without a single logistical worry.
