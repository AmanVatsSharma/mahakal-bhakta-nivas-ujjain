There is something magical about Ujjain during the monsoon. The ancient city, already steeped in spiritual history, takes on an almost ethereal quality when the rains arrive — the Shipra River swells, the temples glisten with wet stone, and the entire landscape turns a vivid, life-affirming green.

It is against this backdrop of monsoon abundance that Hariyali Amavasya arrives — the new moon festival of Shravan that celebrates growth, renewal, and the blessings of Lord Shiva. For pilgrims and travelers, the Hariyali Amavasya Mela at Ram Ghat in Ujjain is one of the most atmospheric and culturally rich events of the Shravan season.

Here is everything you need to know to experience this festival fully.

## What Is Hariyali Amavasya?

The name itself tells you much of the story. "Hariyali" means green — referring to the lush monsoon vegetation that covers the countryside during Shravan. "Amavasya" means new moon — the day of the month when the moon is not visible in the sky. Hariyali Amavasya is the new moon day of the Hindu month of Shravan, when the monsoon greenery is at its most vibrant.

In Hindu tradition, the new moon (Amavasya) is considered a spiritually potent day. For Lord Shiva devotees in particular, the new moon of Shravan holds extra significance. It is believed that performing pujas, rituals, and charitable acts on this day brings amplified blessings. The combination of the green monsoon landscape, the sacred new moon, and the devotion to Lord Shiva makes Hariyali Amavasya a uniquely auspicious occasion.

## Hariyali Amavasya and Lord Shiva

The connection between Hariyali Amavasya and Lord Shiva runs deep. Lord Shiva is the Adi Yogi — the original yogi who lives in the mountains and forests. The lush green landscape of Shravan, when nature is in full bloom, is seen as a manifestation of Shiva's own creative energy.

On Hariyali Amavasya, devotees perform special pujas to Lord Shiva, seeking:
- **Prosperity and growth:** The green monsoon landscape symbolizes material and spiritual growth
- **Protection from evil:** The new moon is considered a time when negative energies are stronger; Shiva's blessings provide protection
- **Wish fulfillment:** Many devotees perform specific rituals on this day to seek the fulfillment of long-held wishes
- **Family welfare:** It is common for families to perform collective pujas for the well-being of all members

In Ujjain, where Mahakaleshwar is the living, breathing presence of Lord Shiva, Hariyali Amavasya takes on an added dimension of significance. The temple becomes the focal point of the city's celebrations.

## The Spiritual Significance of Green (Hariyali)

The "green" in Hariyali Amavasya is not incidental. In Indian spiritual tradition, the color green represents:
- **Growth and abundance:** The monsoon transforms the landscape, and this natural abundance is seen as a divine blessing
- **Vasuki and the nagas:** The green serpents associated with Lord Shiva, particularly the cosmic serpent Vasuki that adorns his neck, connect to the monsoon imagery
- **Life force (prana):** Green is the color of life, vitality, and the life force that flows through all living beings
- **Renewal:** Just as the monsoon renews the earth, Hariyali Amavasya represents spiritual renewal for the devotee

This is why tree worship and plant-based offerings are common on this day — the devotee honors the very manifestation of divine abundance.

## The Hariyali Amavasya Mela at Ram Ghat

The highlight of Hariyali Amavasya in Ujjain is the mela (fair) organized at Ram Ghat on the banks of the Shipra River. The ghat, which is usually a place of quiet contemplation, transforms into a vibrant fairground on this day.

### The Mela Atmosphere

Arriving at Ram Ghat during Hariyali Amavasya is an experience in itself. The ghat is decorated with colorful flags, lights, and flowers. The air is filled with the sounds of devotional songs, the chatter of devotees, and the calls of vendors. The Shipra River, swollen with monsoon waters, adds a dramatic backdrop to the celebrations.

The mela typically features:
- **Temporary shops and stalls:** Selling religious items, puja kits, sweets, snacks, toys, and handicrafts
- **Food stalls:** Offering everything from traditional Ujjain chaat to full meals
- **Bhajan sessions:** Groups of devotional singers perform near the ghat, creating a spiritual musical atmosphere
- **Cultural programs:** Local artists and community groups organize performances
- **Ride and game stalls:** For families with children, making it a festive outing for all ages

The crowd is a mix of serious pilgrims, curious tourists, and local families enjoying the festival atmosphere. While it does get busy, the overall vibe is cheerful and devotional rather than overwhelming.

### The Mela at Night

Ram Ghat takes on a different character after sunset during Hariyali Amavasya. The temporary lighting, the reflections on the water, and the evening aarti create a magical atmosphere. Many pilgrims choose to stay at the ghat into the evening, watching the lamps float on the river and participating in the collective aarti. For the best experience, plan to be at Ram Ghat by 6:00 PM for the evening program.

## Rituals and Ceremonies

The core of Hariyali Amavasya observance in Ujjain revolves around rituals performed at Ram Ghat and Mahakaleshwar Temple:

### Holy Dip in the Shipra

Many devotees begin their Hariyali Amavasya with a dip in the Shipra River at Ram Ghat. The river, considered sacred in Ujjain, is believed to wash away sins and purify the mind and body. The monsoon waters make the dip feel especially refreshing and spiritually cleansing.

The ritual typically follows this pattern:
- Enter the water at the designated bathing area
- Take three dips while chanting "Om Namah Shivaya"
- Offer a floating diya (lamp) or flower to the river
- Dry off and change into clean clothes before proceeding to the temple

### Puja at Ram Ghat

After the river dip, devotees perform pujas at the ghat temples and shrines. Special offerings are made to Lord Shiva and Nag Devta, including flowers, milk, fruits, and coconuts. Priests at the ghat help perform the rituals for those who are unfamiliar with the procedures.

### Mahakaleshwar Temple Visit

The primary destination for most Hariyali Amavasya observers is Mahakaleshwar Temple. Devotees offer special prayers and participate in the day's aarti schedule. Since it is a new moon day, many believe that darshan of Mahakaleshwar on this day carries extra spiritual merit.

### Tree and Plant Worship

Reflecting the "Hariyali" (green) theme, many devotees also perform pujas to trees and plants on this day — particularly the sacred Peepal (Ficus religiosa) and Banyan trees. It is common to see small puja setups at the base of large trees near temples and ghats.

## The Monsoon Advantage

Visiting Ujjain during Hariyali Amavasya means experiencing the city at its most beautiful. The monsoon brings:

- **Cooler temperatures:** Daytime highs of 28-32 degrees instead of the scorching 43+ of summer
- **Lush landscapes:** The city and its temples look their best in the rain
- **Fewer extreme heat concerns:** You can spend more time outdoors at Ram Ghat without exhaustion
- **Atmospheric skies:** Dramatic cloud formations and evening lightning add to the spiritual mood

The trade-off is practical: rain gear, possible travel delays, and slippery ghat steps. But for most pilgrims, the atmospheric reward outweighs the inconvenience.

## Practical Tips for Visiting the Hariyali Amavasya Mela

**Timing is everything.** The mela is at its liveliest from late morning through the evening. Early morning (before 8:00 AM) is quieter and better if you prefer a more peaceful temple visit. The afternoon and early evening see the largest crowds.

**Dress appropriately.** Wear comfortable, modest clothing that you don't mind getting wet. If you plan to take a dip in the Shipra, bring a change of clothes. Many temporary changing facilities are available near the ghat.

**Carry cash.** The mela stalls and many small vendors at Ram Ghat prefer cash over digital payments. Keep small denominations handy.

**Keep your belongings safe.** The mela crowds mean pickpockets can be active. Keep your wallet and phone in secure pockets or a small bag worn close to your body.

**Stay hydrated.** Even in the monsoon, the mela atmosphere can be tiring. Drink water regularly.

**Check the weather.** Heavy monsoon showers are common during this period. Keep an umbrella or raincoat handy and monitor the forecast.
