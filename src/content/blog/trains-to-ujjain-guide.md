For most of India, the yatra to Mahakal begins on rails — Ujjain Junction has been carrying pilgrims into the temple quarter for over a century, and the rail option remains the most restful way to arrive. This is the practical rail map.

## Ujjain Junction (UJN): What to Know

- **Location**: 2–3 km from the Mahakaleshwar temple quarter — autos/e-rickshaws take 10–15 minutes
- **Facilities**: waiting halls, retiring-room style options, cloakroom, food stalls, prepaid-style auto availability
- **Crowd rhythm**: festival evenings and Shravan Mondays swell the station itself — arrive patient
- **Festival note**: on the biggest days, the last road stretch to the temple is pedestrianised — pack accordingly or let your stay handle luggage ([we do](/contact/))

| **Jaipur/Ajmer, Ahmedabad** | Day/overnight options | Good northern & western links |
| **Nagpur/Itarsi, Hyderabad-side** | Connecting/through services | via Itarsi–Bhopal corridor |

Schedules evolve — treat IRCTC/NTES as the source of truth for your date. Recent years added the Shiva-themed **Mahakal Express** on select routes: themed coaches celebrating Mahakaleshwar, worth catching if its schedule matches yours.

## The Overnight-Arrival Playbook

Arriving by night train is a classic Ujjain move — straight from berth to Bhasma Aarti queue. Make it smooth:

1. **Tell your dharamshala your train name/number** — we hold confirmed rooms for late arrivals and track delays ([book on WhatsApp](/rooms/)).
2. **Pick a stay within the walking zone** — station → room → temple, all short hops ([where to stay](/blog/where-to-stay-near-mahakaleshwar-temple/)).
3. **Auto fare**: station → temple quarter is inexpensive; agree the price before boarding or use metered/app options where available.
4. **Keep essentials in one hand-bag** — main luggage goes to the room, you go to the queue.
