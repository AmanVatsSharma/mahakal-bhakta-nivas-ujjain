## Why Ujjain Weddings Need Room Blocks

Individual bookings look harmless until they collide with wedding reality:

- **Guests arrive in waves, not at once.** The core family lands two days early; the baraat convoy arrives on muhurat day; cousins trickle in around both. Ten separate bookings made at ten different times is how a family ends up in three different neighbourhoods.
- **The wedding season is also the darshan season.** November to February is peak for both weddings and pilgrimage — Somvar crowds and marriage parties are literally competing for the same temple-quarter rooms. Verify current availability early; the bands that look open in September look nothing like December.
- **Muhurat timings ignore hotel schedules.** A 3 AM muhurat means guests returning at 5 AM, and a stay that cannot flex around that becomes the weak link in the whole celebration.

A room block — one booking, one coordinator, mixed room types under one roof — solves all three at once.

## The 4 to 8 Week Rule

Work backwards from the muhurat date: **block 8 weeks out for peak-season weddings (November to February), 4 to 6 weeks out for off-season dates**. Before confirming, lock four things in writing — room types and count, nights, vehicle count, and meal requirements. That one message is the difference between a wedding where guests see each other and a wedding where guests commute.
