# Kartik Purnima & Deep Daan Ujjain: Sacred Full Moon Festival at Mahakaleshwar & Ram Ghat

If there is one festival in Ujjain that is pure magic, it is Kartik Purnima. The full moon night of the Hindu month of Kartik — when the moon is at its brightest and most luminous — combined with the sacred Deep Daan ceremony on the Shipra River, creates an experience that borders on the sublime.

Kartik Purnima is not just a festival in Ujjain — it is an event that draws pilgrims from across the country and transforms the city into a living canvas of light, devotion, and collective wonder. Here is your complete guide to experiencing Kartik Purnima and Deep Daan in Ujjain.

## The Significance of Kartik Month and Kartik Purnima

The Hindu month of Kartik (roughly corresponding to October–November) is considered the most sacred month of the entire year. It is the month when the monsoon has receded, the skies are clear, the air is crisp, and the spiritual energy of the earth is believed to be at its peak.

Kartik Purnima — the full moon day of Kartik month — is the culmination of this sacred month. It is one of the most auspicious days in the Hindu calendar, revered by both Lord Vishnu and Lord Shiva devotees. For Lord Vishnu followers, Kartik Purnima commemorates the day Lord Vishnu incarnated as Matsya (the fish avatar) to save humanity from a great deluge. For Lord Shiva devotees, it is a day of intensified devotion and spiritual practice.

In Ujjain, a city where Lord Shiva is the presiding deity, Kartik Purnima draws enormous crowds to Mahakaleshwar Temple. Many devotees observe the entire Kartik month with daily prayers, fasting, and charity, and Kartik Purnima marks the grand conclusion of their spiritual practice.

## Deep Daan: The Ceremony of Floating Lamps

The most visually stunning aspect of Kartik Purnima in Ujjain is Deep Daan. Deep Daan (also called Deepotsav) is the ancient tradition of offering light to the sacred river as a form of worship and gratitude.

On Kartik Purnima, thousands of devotees gather at Ram Ghat on the banks of the Shipra River. Each devotee prepares a small diya — a lamp made of clay or paper, filled with oil and a cotton wick. The diya is lit with a prayer, a wish, or a silent intention, and then gently released into the river. The lamp floats downstream, carried by the gentle current, its small flame flickering against the dark water and the bright full moon.

### The Experience of Deep Daan

The Deep Daan ceremony at Ram Ghat during Kartik Purnima is unlike anything else you will witness. Here is what it feels like:

- **The crowd:** Thousands of people line the steps of Ram Ghat, each holding a lit diya. The atmosphere is one of shared devotion — you are part of a vast, silent collective making an offering of light.
- **The lamps:** The diyas come in all shapes and sizes. Some are simple clay lamps, others are elaborately decorated. Many carry flowers or small offerings alongside the lamp.
- **The river:** The Shipra, calm and dark under the full moon, becomes a river of lights as thousands of lamps drift downstream. The visual is breathtaking — small points of warm light moving slowly across the dark water.
- **The full moon:** Kartik Purnima has one of the brightest full moons of the year. The combination of the moonlight on the water and the floating diyas creates a surreal, almost dreamlike atmosphere.
- **The sounds:** Bhajan singing from the ghat, the gentle lapping of water, the murmurs of devotees — it is a quiet, reverent celebration rather than a loud one.

Many devotees stay at the ghat for hours after releasing their diyas, simply watching the lamps float away and soaking in the spiritual atmosphere. It is a deeply personal moment even in the midst of thousands.

### How to Participate in Deep Daan

Participating in Deep Daan is straightforward but requires a bit of planning:

- **Arrive at Ram Ghat before sunset.** This gives you time to find a spot on the ghat steps and prepare your lamp.
- **Buy or bring your diya.** You can purchase ready-made diyas from vendors near the ghat, or bring your own. Some devotees prefer making their own from clay — it adds a personal touch to the offering.
- **Light your lamp with a prayer.** As you light the diya, silently offer your wish, prayer, or intention. It does not need to be elaborate — sincerity is what matters.
- **Release the lamp carefully.** Gently place the lit diya on the water. Avoid crowding near the water's edge, as the ghat steps can be slippery.
- **Stay and reflect.** After releasing your lamp, find a comfortable spot on the ghat and spend some time in quiet reflection or devotional singing.

## Mahakaleshwar Temple During Kartik Purnima

While Deep Daan at Ram Ghat is the highlight of Kartik Purnima, Mahakaleshwar Temple is equally significant on this day. The temple follows an extended schedule:

- **Mangla Aarti:** Early morning (around 4:00 AM) — the first aarti of the most auspicious full moon day
- **Bhasma Aarti:** Multiple slots throughout the day — this is the most sought-after darshan on Kartik Purnima
- **Regular aartis:** Every two hours as per the regular schedule, with larger crowds
- **Sandhya Aarti:** The evening aarti coincides with the sunset and the lead-up to Deep Daan
- **Shayan Aarti:** The final aarti before the temple closes for the night

Many devotees follow a routine of performing Mahakaleshwar darshan during the day and then heading to Ram Ghat for the evening Deep Daan ceremony. This combination of temple worship and river ritual makes for a complete spiritual experience.

## The Kartik Purnima Mela at Ram Ghat

Alongside the Deep Daan ceremony, a vibrant mela springs up around Ram Ghat during Kartik Purnima. The mela adds a festive, community dimension to the spiritual event:

- **Religious item stalls:** Selling diyas, puja kits, flowers, and prasad items
- **Food stalls:** Traditional snacks, chaat, and hot beverages — perfect for the cool Kartik evening
- **Handicraft vendors:** Local artisans sell handmade items, adding to the festive atmosphere
- **Cultural performances:** Some years, local cultural groups organize devotional music and dance performances near the ghat
- **Community gatherings:** Many local families and groups come together at the ghat for the evening, making it a social as much as a spiritual occasion

The mela typically begins a day or two before Kartik Purnima and continues for a couple of days after. If you are in Ujjain during this period, the Ram Ghat area is definitely worth exploring beyond the main ceremony.

## Kartik Purnima in Ujjain vs. Other Full Moon Days

If you have experienced a regular full moon night at Ram Ghat, Kartik Purnima will feel dramatically different:

- **Scale of Deep Daan:** The number of lamps released on Kartik Purnima dwarfs any other day. What might be a few hundred lamps on a regular full moon becomes tens of thousands on Kartik Purnima.
- **Crowd size:** The ghat and temple areas are packed with devotees from across the country. Arriving early is essential.
- **Festive decorations:** Ram Ghat and Mahakaleshwar Temple are elaborately decorated for Kartik Purnima — far beyond their usual adornments.
- **Special arrangements:** The temple administration and local authorities set up extensive crowd management, lighting, and safety arrangements for the Deep Daan night.
- **Spiritual intensity:** The collective energy of thousands of devotees performing Deep Daan together creates a spiritual intensity that is genuinely unique.

## The Mythology of Deep Daan

The tradition of Deep Daan is rooted in one of Hinduism's most powerful symbols: light as an offering. The lamp (diya) represents the inner light of consciousness — the atman that is eternal and undying. Releasing it on a sacred river is an act of surrender: you are offering your deepest self to the divine current that flows through all existence.

The Shipra River is considered especially potent for this offering. As one of the rivers where amrita (nectar of immortality) fell during the Samudra Manthan, the Shipra is already charged with divine energy. Floating a lamp on the Shipra is not just a beautiful gesture — it is an offering made to a river that is literally infused with the nectar of the gods.

## Deep Daan Through the Year: Not Just Kartik Purnima

While Kartik Purnima is the most famous Deep Daan night, the tradition continues throughout the year on every full moon (Purnima). If you are in Ujjain on a regular full moon night, Ram Ghat still sees hundreds of lamps released — it is simply smaller and more intimate than the Kartik Purnima version.

Similarly, major festivals like **Diwali** and **Deepotsav** (the festival of lights) see large-scale Deep Daan ceremonies at Ram Ghat. During **Mahashivratri**, the corridor and ghats are illuminated with thousands of diyas as part of the all-night vigil. Each of these nights offers a different flavour of the same ritual.

## The Kartik Month: A Spiritual Marathon

Kartik Purnima is not just a one-night event — it is the culmination of a full month of spiritual practice. The entire month of Kartik (October–November) is considered the most sacred month in the Hindu calendar, and many devotees observe specific practices throughout:

- **Daily temple visits**: Many devotees visit Mahakaleshwar every morning throughout Kartik month
- **Fasting on Ekadashi**: The 11th day of each lunar fortnight is observed with fasting
- **Kartik vrata**: Some devotees observe a daily fast with only one meal, typically in the evening
- **Deep Daan every evening**: Some families release a lamp on the Shipra every evening of Kartik month, building up to the grand finale on Purnima
- **Charity and seva**: Donating food, clothes, and helping the needy is considered especially meritorious during Kartik

By the time Kartik Purnima arrives, the spiritual energy built up over a month of practice makes the final night extraordinary. Many devotees describe Kartik Purnima not as a single event but as the explosion of a month-long accumulation of devotion.
