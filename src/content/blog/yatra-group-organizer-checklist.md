Every **yatra group planning** conversation starts with rooms and ends with the realisation that rooms were the easy part. Taking twenty, forty or sixty pilgrims to Mahakaleshwar means managing headcounts, buses, meal preferences, aarti registrations and elders' stamina — all inside a temple town that runs on its own clock. This is the **temple group travel in Ujjain** checklist we hand organizers who book with us, compiled from the trips that ran smoothly and the ones that taught us what a missing line item costs. It deliberately covers the logistics half of the trip; the rooms half has its own detailed guide in our [group booking post for Ujjain](/blog/group-booking-ujjain-dharamshala/).

## Start With the Rooms: Blocked by Type, Not Hope

The foundation of any **bus group in Ujjain** is a room block confirmed by type, count and night — not a verbal "we will manage". For a 20-person yatra, a mix that works in practice:

| Room type | Rooms | Sleeps | Nightly cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 Bed AC | 4 | 16 | ₹9,900 |
| 2 Bed AC | 2 | 4 | ₹3,300 |
| **Total** | **6 rooms** | **20** | **₹13,200 — about ₹660 per head** |

Swap in 4 Bed Non-AC (₹2,100) in the cool season and the same headcount drops to roughly ₹545 per person. Confirm the mix in writing, name each room type, and reconfirm a week out. Elders get lower floors and attached hot-water bathrooms as a rule, not a favour.

## Bus Parking and Arrival Timing

A 40-seat coach changes the physics of the temple quarter — lanes near the gate were not built for it. Plan the arrival like a landing sequence:

- **Call ahead with the bus size and ETA.** We coordinate arrival slots so the coach drops the group at the door and parks where large vehicles fit, farther out.
- **Schedule arrivals outside darshan peaks.** A bus unloading during a Somvar surge creates a bottleneck that eats an hour of your buffer.
- **Keep the driver's number** in your coordination group, with the pickup time agreed the night before departure day.

## Meal Counts: Veg, Jain and Avoid Lists

Group eating fails on missing information, not missing food. Collect exactly three numbers before the trip:

1. **Total veg headcount** — the base number for every meal.
2. **Jain and onion-garlic-free count** — common in Malwa kitchens, but only cookable if the kitchen knows in advance. Tell the kitchen your dietary practice and it will try to accommodate.
3. **Avoid lists** — allergies, diabetic-friendly needs, no-spice requirements for elders.

Give our kitchen the final consolidated count the evening before each meal day, plus meal timings aligned to your darshan plan. One WhatsApp message does it; the alternative is a scrum of individual requests at the dining door.

## Aarti Passes and Darshan Registration in Advance

The paperwork items that sink more group plans than any other:

- **Bhasma Aarti** requires advance registration and carry original photo ID for every attendee — collect IDs at boarding, not at the temple gate.
- **Darshan and aarti passes** for peak days (Somvar, Shravan, festival dates) should be arranged well ahead; our desk guides organizers through the current process. For the seasonal dynamics, read our [Shravan Somvar guide for Mahakaleshwar](/blog/shravan-somvar-mahakaleshwar-guide/).
- **Lockers and shoe stands** scale badly with group size — factor 15 minutes per standstill, not 5.

## The Headcount Buddy System

Twenty-plus people cannot be supervised directly by one organizer. Distribute the task:

- **Pair every pilgrim with a buddy.** Buddies confirm each other at every assembly point — no exceptions, including the confident uncle who "was just stepping out for tea".
- **Count at every transition:** bus boarding, temple entry, temple exit, meal start, day end. Six counts a day, each taking ninety seconds.
- **Assign floaters** — one responsible person per eight pilgrims — for elder assistance and restroom pauses during queues.

## First Aid and the Elders Queue Plan

The kit that earns its bag: band-aids, ORS sachets, paracetamol, antacid, motion-sickness tablets, a tube of antiseptic, and each elder's personal medication list with dosages. For elders:

- **Plan the shorter darshan route** and use whatever senior-citizen assistance the temple offers on the day — verify current provisions before travel.
- **Schedule rest hours deliberately** — a group that rests between the morning and evening aarti survives day two; a group that does not, does not.
- **Keep one room as a recovery base** with hot water available for the ones who fade mid-day.

## Buffer Time and the One-Group Rule

Two closing disciplines separate calm yatras from chaotic ones:

1. **The half-again rule.** Take every time estimate and add fifty percent — a 90-minute darshan plan gets 135 minutes. Queues, headcounts and shoe stands scale with group size, not intentions.
2. **One WhatsApp group with our desk.** Room changes, meal counts, arrival ETAs, aarti updates — everything flows through one channel that includes our front desk. Fragmented coordination is how groups lose hours.
