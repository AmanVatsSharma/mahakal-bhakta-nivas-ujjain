Before a wedding invitation is printed, before a new shop opens its shutters, before a family steps over the threshold of a new home — in countless Indian households, one phone call precedes all of these: the call to the family astrologer. And when the advice comes back, it often includes a pooja. **Navagraha shanti pooja in Ujjain** is among the most requested rituals at our desk, because this city occupies a rare position in the tradition of the planets: it is at once a city of Shiva and a city of the stars.

Here is what the pooja means, why Ujjain matters for it, and how families arrange it.

## The Nine Grahas in Tradition

In jyotish, the **nine grahas** — Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Rahu and Ketu — are read as nine influences that colour a life. They are not all "planets" in the modern sense; two are shadow points of the eclipse. **Navagraha dosh nivaran** is sought when one or several of these are read as afflicting a chart — a difficult Saturn period, a strong Mars placement, an eclipse-born horoscope. Rather than prescribing fear, the tradition prescribes shanti: pacification through worship, fire and giving.

A framing we stand by: devotees believe the grahas respond to reverence. This is a tradition of coming into harmony with time, not of bargaining with fate.

## Why Ujjain: the City of Time and the Birthplace of Mars

Two things give Ujjain its standing for graha shanti. First, this is one of the great historical seats of Indian astronomy — the city through which tradition drew its prime meridian, home to the Jantar Mantar observatory raised by Maharaja Jai Singh. A city that spent centuries measuring the heavens is a natural home for poojas that address them.

Walk into the Jantar Mantar off the old city lanes and you are looking at instruments built to read the sky three centuries ago — sundials the height of houses, walls aligned to the movement of stars. Ujjain was for centuries the reference line of traditional Hindu geography, the meridian from which positions were reckoned. That heritage is not museum trivia here; it is why jyotish and this city grew up together, and why a graha shanti arranged in Ujjain comes with a certain depth of scholarship behind its priestly families.

Second, and dearer to devotees: **Mangalnath, on the banks of the Shipra, is revered as the birthplace of Mangal — Mars himself**. For anyone whose chart turns on Mars, worship here carries a particular intimacy. Add Mahakal, the lord of time, and you begin to see why families traveling for graha shanti so often choose Ujjain over larger cities.

## How the Navagraha Homa Works

The vidhi typically unfolds like this:

- **Invocation of each graha** with its own mantra, often before nine small mounds or kalashas representing the nine
- **The homa** — offerings of ghee, specific grains and sacred woods into the fire, one round for each graha
- **Daan** — gifts of navadhanya (nine grains), cloth and other items traditionally associated with the planets, closing the ritual

The whole pooja usually takes a few hours in the morning. The priest speaks each graha name through the sankalp, and families typically bring the horoscope so the emphasis falls on the planets the astrologer flagged.

## Pairing the Pooja With a Mangalnath Visit

If Mars sits at the centre of your concern, do the shanti and then visit Mangalnath. The temple sits a short drive from the main cluster and is calmer than Mahakal, which families with elders appreciate. ## When Families Book It

- **Before weddings** — the classic occasion, with both charts read together
- **New ventures** — shops, offices, professional beginnings
- **Griha pravesh** — entering a newly built or bought home
- **Whenever the family priest advises it** — often around a Saturn period or an eclipse-born chart

## Practicalities at a Glance

- **Duration:** a full homa with sankalp and daan takes about half a morning; keep three to four hours free
- **Best hour:** mornings are traditional for graha poojas, and the temple lane is cooler then
- **Booking:** share the chart and purpose with our desk a day or two ahead, so the priest prepares the right samagri
- **Afterwards:** elders and children will want rest, hot food and no long walk — plan the day around the pooja, not around the traffic

## What to Bring

- The horoscope of the person — of both partners, for a wedding
- Names and gotra for the sankalp
- Simple clothes suited to sitting near a homa
- If you wish, a note from your astrologer naming the grahas to emphasise
