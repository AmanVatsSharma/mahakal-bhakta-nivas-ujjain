Ujjain does not observe its festivals — it *inhabits* them. On the big days, the temple quarter becomes a sea of devotees, the Shipra brims with bathers, and the city's ancient rhythms take over completely. Whether you want to join the swell or dodge it, this calendar tells you what happens when — and how to plan around it.

## The Big Days, Ranked by Intensity

| Festival | When | Crowd level | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simhastha Kumbh** | Once in 12 yrs (next **2028**) | Beyond scale | ~12 crore devotees across the mela; Shahi Snans in the Shipra ([guide](/blog/kumbh-mela-ujjain-2028-guide/)) |
| **Mahashivratri** | Feb–Mar | Extreme | The wedding night of Shiva; night-long worship, lakhs in queue ([guide](/blog/mahashivratri-mahakaleshwar-guide/)) |
| **Shravan Somvar** | Mondays of Shravan (Jul–Aug) | Very heavy | Kanwars and special Somvar worship ([Shravan guide](/blog/shravan-somvar-mahakaleshwar-guide/)) |
| **Hariyali Amavasya** | Shravan new moon | Very heavy | Shipra snan from pre-dawn; ghat-side rituals citywide |
| **Nag Panchami** | Shravan Shukla Panchami | Heavy | Ujjain's signature Nag worship; distinctive local observances |
| **Purnima (full moon)** | Monthly | Moderate–heavy | Elevated footfall at temple and ghats |
| **Normal weekday** | — | Light | The darshan window regulars cherish |

## Nag Panchami — Ujjain's Own

Among Ujjain's festivals, Nag Panchami holds a special local flavour: serpent deities receive worship with milk and special rituals, homes and temples fashion protective Nag icons, and Mahakaleshwar sees a deep surge of local devotees. If you are in Ujjain then, arrive early — the temple quarter's lanes fill by mid-morning.

## Hariyali Amavasya — The Green New Moon

Falling in the monsoon month of Shravan, Hariyali Amavasya sends devotees to the **Shipra ghats before dawn** for the sacred bath, followed by temple rounds. Ram Ghat and adjoining ghats are thronged from the early hours; the evening lamp-floating is visually beautiful. Vehicle access near the ghats narrows — another day when walking-distance accommodation pays for itself ([see rooms](/rooms/)).

## Planning Rules for Festival Travel

1. **Rooms first, tickets second.** On big days, trains and buses somehow get managed; walking-distance rooms do not. Book 4–8 weeks ahead (Shivratri, Nag Panchami) or months ahead (Shravan weekends).
2. **Shift by ±1 day.** The day before a festival is often calm; the day after, tired-calm. The festival day itself is for immersion — go only if your heart is set on it.
3. **Elders and festivals don't mix well in queues.** If travelling with seniors, prefer the livestream-able energy of the *eve* and do darshan the following morning.
4. **Cash small, phone charged, one bag.** Festival-quarter logistics in one line.
5. **Trust your hosts for ground truth.** Festival-day crowd patterns shift year to year; ask us on [WhatsApp](/rooms/) when booking — we will tell you what the queue looked like last time.

## The Quiet-Seeker's Calendar

If your goal is undisturbed darshan and short queues:

- **Best windows**: Tuesday–Friday, non-festival weeks, October–March
- **Best hours**: 6:00–8:00 AM, or after 8:30 PM
- **Avoid**: the entire table above
