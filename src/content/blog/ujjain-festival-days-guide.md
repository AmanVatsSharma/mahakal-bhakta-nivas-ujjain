Ujjain does not merely observe its festivals — it inhabits them. On the big days, the temple quarter becomes a living sea of devotion, the Shipra brims with bathers from before dawn, and the city's ancient rhythms take over completely. Whether you want to join the swell or plan your visit around the quieter days, this guide maps out every major festival, its significance, crowd expectations, and how to make the most of it.

## Major Festivals at Mahakaleshwar: The Complete Calendar

### Mahashivratri — The Night of Shiva

**When**: February–March (the 14th day of the dark fortnight of Magha month)

**Significance**: The night believed to be when Lord Shiva performed the cosmic dance of creation, preservation, and destruction. It is also considered the night of Shiva and Parvati's marriage. Mahashivratri at Mahakaleshwar is the most powerful spiritual night of the year.

**What happens**: The temple opens at midnight (or earlier) and remains open through the night. Lakhs of devotees wait in queues that can stretch for kilometres. A special **Rudra Abhishek** is performed throughout the night. The entire temple quarter glows with diyas, flowers, and chant. The energy is unlike any other night in Ujjain.

**Crowd level**: Extreme. Plan for long queues, restricted entry points, and heavy local traffic.

**Booking tip**: Book rooms at least 2–3 months in advance. The entire city sells out. See [our rooms page](/rooms/) for festival availability.

**Aarti**: Special night-long aarti schedule. Arrive by 10 PM for a manageable queue.

### Shravan Month (Sawan) — The Month of Shiva

**When**: July–August (the fifth month of the Hindu calendar)

**Significance**: Shravan is the most sacred month for Shiva devotees. Every Monday (Somvar) during Shravan is considered especially auspicious, and devotees observe fasts, perform abhishek, and carry holy water from the Ganga on foot as Kanwars.

**What happens**: Every Somvar in Shravan sees a massive influx of Kanwar yatris — devotees who walk hundreds of kilometres carrying Ganga water for abhishek at Mahakaleshwar. The Shipra ghats are busy from pre-dawn. Special aartis are held throughout the month.

**Crowd level**: Very heavy on Mondays, moderate on other days.

**Booking tip**: Mondays in Shravan require advance booking — see our [Shravan Somvar guide](/blog/shravan-somvar-mahakaleshwar-guide/) and [stay during Shravan guide](/blog/stay-during-shravan-ujjain/).

### Hariyali Amavasya

**When**: New moon day in Shravan (July–August)

**Significance**: The no-moon day of Shravan, dedicated to Lord Shiva. Devotees bathe in the Shipra before sunrise and perform rituals for peace and prosperity. The riverfront comes alive with thousands of early-morning bathers.

**What happens**: Pre-dawn bathing at the Shipra ghats, special pujas at Mahakaleshwar, and community feasts. The ghats fill up from 3 AM onwards.

**Crowd level**: Very heavy, especially at the ghats.

### Nag Panchami

**When**: Fifth day of the bright fortnight of Shravan (July–August)

**Significance**: Dedicated to the worship of Nag (serpent) devatas, which hold special significance in Shiva tradition. Ujjain celebrates this with particular devotion.

**What happens**: Special Nag worship ceremonies at temples across the city, including at Mahakaleshwar. Traditional Nag idols are worshipped with milk, flowers, and sindoor. The festival has a distinctive local character in Ujjain.

**Crowd level**: Heavy at temple locations.

### Simhastha Kumbh Mela

**When**: Once every 12 years (next: 2028)

**Significance**: The grandest of all — a month-long spiritual gathering of crores of devotees, Shahi Snan dates, Akhara processions, and continuous religious activity along the Shipra. See our [dedicated guide](/blog/kumbh-mela-ujjain-2028-guide/) for full planning information.

**What happens**: The entire city transforms. Temporary tent cities, riverfront ghats expanded to lakhs of bathing capacity, processions of Naga sadhus, continuous aarti at Mahakaleshwar, and spiritual discourses across hundreds of camps.

**Crowd level**: Beyond any normal scale. Plan extensively.

### Navratri

**When**: Twice a year — Chaitra Navratri (March–April) and Sharad Navratri (September–October). Sharad Navratri is the larger celebration.

**Significance**: Nine nights dedicated to the nine forms of Goddess Durga. While Mahakaleshwar is a Shiva temple, Ujjain celebrates Navratri with equal fervour. The Harsiddhi Temple (one of Ujjain's 51 Shakti Peethas) becomes the focal point, but Mahakaleshwar also holds special events.

**What happens**: Garba and dandiya nights, special Durga aartis, decorated temple complexes, and cultural programmes across the city. The Harsiddhi Temple sees particularly heavy crowds.

**Crowd level**: Heavy, especially on the final three nights.

**Booking tip**: Sharad Navratri (around October) is peak tourist season. Book 1–2 months ahead.

### Diwali and Annakut

**When**: October–November (based on the lunar calendar)

**Significance**: Diwali at Mahakaleshwar is spectacular. The temple is decorated with thousands of diyas, and the Annakut (mountain of food) offering the next day is one of the most visually impressive events at the temple — hundreds of dishes offered to the deity.

**What happens**: The entire temple quarter glows with lights. The Annakut features a stunning display of food offerings, each prepared with devotion. The atmosphere is festive and deeply devotional simultaneously.

**Crowd level**: Very heavy, especially on Diwali evening and Annakut morning.

### Kartik Purnima and Deepdaan

**When**: Full moon day of Kartik month (October–November)

**Significance**: The full moon of Kartik is one of the most auspicious days of the year. Devotees perform Deepdaan (lamp offering) on the Shipra, releasing thousands of diyas into the river at sunset. The sight of the river lit with floating lamps is extraordinary.

**What happens**: Evening Deepdaan ceremony at Ram Ghat and other ghats, special aarti at Mahakaleshwar, and community celebrations. The Shipra becomes a river of lights.

**Crowd level**: Heavy at the ghats in the evening.

**If you want to experience the full energy**: Plan around Mahashivratri or a major festival. The intensity is unforgettable. Just be prepared for crowds, book well in advance, and pace yourself — the temple quarter will be non-stop activity from dawn to past midnight.
