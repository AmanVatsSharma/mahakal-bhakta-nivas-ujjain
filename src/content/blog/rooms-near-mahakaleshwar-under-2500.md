## What Each Extra Rupee Actually Buys

### ₹1,250 to ₹1,650: Air Conditioning (2 Bed Rooms)

The ₹400 step from the 2 Bed Non-AC to the 2 Bed AC buys exactly one thing: **sleep during the Ujjain summer**. From late March to June, daytime temperatures routinely push into the forties and nights stay stubbornly warm. If you are waking at 3:45 AM for Bhasma Aarti, the quality of those five hours of sleep matters more than anything else in your trip budget. In winter — October to February — skip this upgrade; nights are genuinely cool and a non-AC room is perfectly comfortable.

### ₹1,785 and ₹2,050: The Third Bed

For a group of three, one 3 Bed Non-AC at ₹1,785 (or ₹2,050 with AC) beats two double rooms on every count: total price, luggage logistics, and the simple comfort of keeping the family in one room. The non-AC version is one of the quietest bargains on our list for October-to-February trips.

### ₹2,100 and ₹2,475: The Family Configuration

Four beds in one room is where families win. The **4 Bed Non-AC at ₹2,100** is, per person, the cheapest stay in the entire house. The **4 Bed AC at ₹2,475** is the same room reimagined for hot months — four tired pilgrims, one cool room, no one negotiating over the fan.

## The Per-Person Maths That Changes the Decision

Ranking rooms by headline price hides the real story. Divide by heads and the ladder reshuffles:

- **2 Bed AC** — ₹1,650 ÷ 2 = **₹825 per person**
- **3 Bed AC** — ₹2,050 ÷ 3 = **about ₹683 per person**
- **4 Bed Non-AC** — ₹2,100 ÷ 4 = **₹525 per person**
- **4 Bed AC** — ₹2,475 ÷ 4 = **about ₹619 per person**

The pattern is simple: **the biggest room is often the cheapest per person**. A family of four that instinctively books two doubles can save a significant amount — and gain a shared room — by taking a single 4 Bed instead.
