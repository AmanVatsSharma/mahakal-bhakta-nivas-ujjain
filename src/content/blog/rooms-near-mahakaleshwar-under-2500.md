## Room Features: What to Inspect on Arrival

When you check in, spend two minutes confirming these basics in every room. It takes almost no time and saves any surprise later.

**Bathroom and water:** Turn on both the hot and cold taps. Confirm that the geyser or instant-heat system produces reliably hot water — this is especially important in winter months when demand on shared geysers peaks. Check that the shower drains properly and that the toilet flushes without holding the handle down.

**Air conditioning:** If you booked an AC room, switch it on and walk to the far side of the room. The vent should reach every corner. If the room has a split unit, confirm both the temperature display and the remote respond. In summer, a room that only cools the area immediately around the bed is frustrating at 4 AM.

**WiFi:** Ask the front desk for the current network name and password, then connect your phone. Walk to the window or the door — signal strength varies by room position. If you are travelling for work and need a reliable connection, test it in the corner where you plan to sit.

**Beds and bedding:** Pull back the sheet slightly to confirm the mattress protector is clean. Check that the pillowcase is fresh and the blanket (if provided) has been laundered. This is not paranoia — it is a thirty-second check that costs nothing and gives you peace of mind.

**Windows and ventilation:** For non-AC rooms, open the window fully and check that it locks securely. Verify that the room has adequate cross-ventilation — at least two openings on different walls. In Ujjain's heat, a non-AC room with only one window can feel like an oven by midday even in shoulder season.

**Power backup:** The room should have at least one working lamp or charging point that stays live during power cuts. Ask which MCB or fuse box serves the room if you are unsure. This is particularly relevant for summer stays when Ujjain experiences occasional load shedding.

**Luggage space:** Confirm that the wardrobe or hanger rack has enough hooks and shelf space for your group's clothes. A family of four with a week's worth of clothes needs more than two hangers and one shelf.

**Mosquito netting or coils:** If you are staying in a non-AC room during monsoon or post-monsoon months (July to September), confirm whether the room includes a mosquito net or whether the front desk provides coils. Ask proactively — it is better than discovering the problem at bedtime.

The published tariffs on our [rooms page](/rooms/) are standard-year-round prices, but your actual out-of-pocket cost varies with the season in ways that affect more than just your comfort.

**March to June (summer):** Daytime temperatures regularly exceed 42 degrees Celsius. Non-AC rooms are functional but uncomfortable for anything more than a quick rest. The ₹400 upgrade to AC becomes worth every rupee during these months. Bookings are lighter than winter, so last-minute rooms are usually available — but if you are visiting during school summer holidays (mid-April to mid-June), book a week ahead.

**July to September (monsoon):** Ujjain's monsoon is moderate compared to coastal India, but July and August see daily afternoon showers that make the walk to the temple slightly less pleasant. Room prices stay stable, availability is excellent, and the temple surroundings look spectacular in the rain. The downside is occasional waterlogging on Gopal Mandir Road, so confirm with the front desk whether the access route is clear before you arrive.

**Simhastha (once every 12 years):** The next Simhastha after 2026 will be in 2038. During Simhastha, demand exceeds supply by a huge margin and many hotels raise prices dramatically.
