Ujjain is one of the seven sacred cities of India and a major centre of Vedic astrology.
Among its many revered shrines, the **Navagraha Temple** holds a unique place for devotees
seeking the blessings of the nine celestial planets. If you are planning a pilgrimage to
Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga, adding the Navagraha Temple to your itinerary can bring deeper
spiritual meaning to your visit.

## What Is the Navagraha Temple Ujjain?

The **Navagraha Temple** in Ujjain is dedicated to the nine planetary deities known as the
Navagraha in Hindu tradition. These are **Surya (the Sun), Chandra (the Moon), Mangal
(Mars), Budha (Mercury), Brihaspati (Jupiter), Shukra (Venus), Shani (Saturn), Rahu, and
Ketu**. Each planet is believed to influence different aspects of human life — health,
wealth, relationships, career, and spiritual progress. When a planet is unfavourably placed
in a person's horoscope, devotees visit the Navagraha Temple to offer prayers, perform
rituals, and seek relief.

Ujjain has long been recognised as a premier centre for astrology in India. The ancient
**Vedh Shala (Jantar Mantar)** built by Maharaja Jai Singh II still stands as proof of
Ujjain's deep connection with astronomy and the stars. The Navagraha Temple draws on this
cosmic tradition and is considered one of the most powerful planetary temples in North
India, attracting devotees from across the country.

## Historical and Spiritual Significance

Ujjain, historically known as **Avantika**, has been associated with planetary worship for
over two thousand years. Ancient texts describe Ujjain as a city where the science of
Jyotisha (astrology) flourished under the patronage of great kings like Vikramaditya. The
city's geographical location on the Tropic of Cancer made it a natural hub for astronomical
observation in ancient India.

The Navagraha Temple's design reflects the cosmic order — each planet has a separate shrine
within the complex arranged according to Vedic principles. According to tradition,
**Saturday (Shani day)** and **Tuesday (Mangal day)** are the most auspicious days to
visit. However, devotees visit year-round, especially during **Amavasya (new moon)** and
**Ekadashi**, to perform special prayers for planetary peace.

## Temple Timings and Entry

The Navagraha Temple is open to all visitors and does not charge an entry fee:

- **Morning hours**: 5:00 AM – 12:00 PM
- **Evening hours**: 4:00 PM – 9:00 PM
- **Entry fee**: Free (voluntary donations accepted)

The temple atmosphere is most peaceful during early morning hours, just after 5:00 AM.
Many devotees perform the **Navagraha Shanti Pooja** with the help of a local priest who
can be hired at the temple itself. You can also book **abhishek** (ritual bathing) for a
specific planet that is causing concern in your horoscope. The full pooja typically takes
between 2 and 3 hours and includes **homa (fire ritual)**, mantra chanting, and offerings
to all nine planetary deities.

## How to Reach Navagraha Temple Ujjain

The temple is conveniently located and easy to reach from Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga:

- **From Mahakaleshwar Temple**: approximately 1.5 km — a 20-minute walk or a 5-minute
  auto-rickshaw ride costing Rs. 50 to Rs. 100
- **From Ujjain Railway Station**: about 3 km — 10 minutes by auto or local bus
- **From the Shipra River ghats**: located close to Ram Ghat, easy to combine with a
  riverside walk
- **From Indore**: approximately 55 km — about 1.5 hours by road, making it a popular
  day trip

For first-time visitors, combine this temple with the detailed **[Mahakaleshwar Darshan
Guide](/blog/mahakaleshwar-darshan-guide/)** for a complete spiritual day plan covering
entry gates, queue management, dress code, and crowd tips.

## What to Expect Inside the Temple

The temple complex has nine small shrines, each housing the idol or symbolic representation
of one graha. The architecture is simple and traditional, with stone platforms, brass lamps,
and vivid colours. Inside the main hall, you will find:

- Individual **Navanakshatra (nine-star) yantras** for each planet
- A central shrine of Lord Shiva, the presiding deity of the complex
- **Prasad counters** selling traditional offerings like coconut, flowers, and sesame seeds
- Space for personal **japa (mantra chanting)** — especially the powerful *Navagraha Stotra*
- Clean marble flooring and shaded rest areas for elderly devotees

The temple sees a steady flow of visitors throughout the day. Saturdays are particularly
busy as Shani devotees throng the shrine. If you want a quieter experience, visit on a
Wednesday (Budha day) or a weekday morning.

## The Navagraha Shanti Pooja: What You Need to Know

If you are seeking relief from planetary doshas or going through a challenging phase, the
**Navagraha Shanti Pooja** performed at this temple is highly recommended. Here is what
the ritual typically includes:

- **Kalash Sthapana** — consecration of the sacred water pot
- **Abhishek** — ritual bathing of each planetary deity with milk, honey, and holy water
- **Homa (fire ritual)** — offerings into the sacred fire with specific mantras
- **Navagraha Stotra recitation** — chanting of the ancient planetary hymn
- **Prasad distribution** — blessed food offered to all nine planets
- **Dakshina to priests** — customary donation for the performing priest

The cost typically ranges from **Rs. 501 to Rs. 2,001** depending on the scale of the
pooja. A simpler abhishek for one planet costs around Rs. 251. All items are arranged by
the temple priests, so you do not need to bring anything except your devotion.

## Nearby Attractions to Combine with Your Visit

Ujjain is packed with sacred sites, and the Navagraha Temple sits in good company:

- **Mangalnath Temple** — dedicated to Mangal (Mars), about 1 km away
- **Harsiddhi Temple** — a revered Devi shrine, approximately 2 km from the Navagraha
  Temple
- **Ram Ghat** — the main bathing ghat on the Shipra River, ideal for an evening dip
- **Vedh Shala (Jantar Mantar)** — the ancient observatory, just a short distance away
- **Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga** — the main temple, 1.5 km away

Many pilgrims plan a **single-day temple circuit** covering Mahakaleshwar, Navagraha,
Mangalnath, and Ram Ghat. Start early morning at Mahakaleshwar, visit Navagraha by 9:00
AM, then conclude with a holy dip at Ram Ghat before sunset.

## Practical Tips for Devotees

- **Dress modestly** — wear comfortable cotton clothes; avoid shorts, sleeveless tops, and
  leather items
- **Carry small change** for prasad, donations, and auto fares — many vendors do not accept
  digital payments
- **Photography** is generally allowed in the outer areas but always check with temple
  authorities before clicking inside the sanctum
- **Remove footwear** before entering the inner shrine area; there is a designated shoe
  counter near the entrance
- **Visit during Amavasya** for a special spiritual experience — the energy and crowd are
  both intense but deeply rewarding
- **Stay hydrated** — Ujjain summers are harsh; carry water if visiting between March
  and June
- **Plan accommodation early** during festival seasons like Mahashivratri, Kumbh Mela, or
  Simhastha when demand spikes

## Best Days and Festival Times to Visit

While the Navagraha Temple is open year-round, certain days carry extra significance:

- **Amavasya (New Moon)** — the most powerful day for planetary prayers
- **Makar Sankranti** — Sun God transitions into Capricorn; special Surya worship
- **Maha Shivaratri** — the temple remains open all night with special abhishek
- **Simhastha / Kumbh Mela** — the temple gets extra decorations and extended timings
- **Every Saturday** — especially for Shani devotees; arrive before 8:00 AM for shorter
  queues
- **Every Tuesday** — Mangal pooja day, popular among those seeking courage and energy

During festival times, the temple is beautifully decorated with flowers and lights. Special
community meals (bhandaras) are often organised near the temple premises, and priests offer
group poojas that can be joined by multiple families together at a reduced cost.

## The Mythology Behind Navagraha Worship

According to the **Markandeya Purana** and **Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra**, the Navagraha
deities emerged from the cosmic energies of the universe and govern the destinies of all
living beings. Each planet is associated with a specific deity, colour, and day of the week:

- **Surya (Sun)** — Lord of soul and vitality; worship on Sundays
- **Chandra (Moon)** — Lord of mind and emotions; worship on Mondays
- **Mangal (Mars)** — Lord of energy and courage; worship on Tuesdays
- **Budha (Mercury)** — Lord of intellect and communication; worship on Wednesdays
- **Brihaspati (Jupiter)** — Lord of wisdom and fortune; worship on Thursdays
- **Shukra (Venus)** — Lord of love and prosperity; worship on Fridays
- **Shani (Saturn)** — Lord of discipline and karma; worship on Saturdays
- **Rahu and Ketu** — Lunar nodes associated with spiritual growth and challenges

At the Ujjain Navagraha Temple, each deity is represented by a distinct icon and can be
worshipped individually or collectively. Many devotees perform a **108-round mala japa**
of the Navagraha mantra for lasting peace. The temple priest can also prepare a personalised
**graha dosha nivaran** plan based on your birth chart (kundali), which may include specific
gemstone recommendations, fasting days, and mantra schedules.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is the Navagraha Temple the same as Mangalnath Temple?**

No. Mangalnath Temple is dedicated specifically to Mangal (Mars) and is believed to be the
birthplace of Mars in astrological tradition. The Navagraha Temple houses all nine planetary
deities in one complex. Both temples are in Ujjain and are often visited together on the
same day.

**Q: Which planet's shrine should I visit first?**

Traditionally, devotees begin with **Surya (Sun)** and proceed clockwise through all nine
shrines. However, many people visit only the shrine of the planet that is causing dosha in
their horoscope. A local priest can guide you based on your kundali and recommend the
right sequence of prayers.

**Q: Can I perform Navagraha Shanti Pooja without a prior booking?**

Yes, most priests are available on-site without booking. During festivals like
Mahashivratri, Kumbh Mela, or Simhastha, it is advisable to reach early morning or book a
day in advance to secure a priest and a slot.

**Q: What is the best time of year to visit?**

The months of **October to March** offer the most pleasant weather for temple visits in
Ujjain. Daytime temperatures during this period range from 15 to 30 degrees Celsius.
Avoid visiting during peak summer (April–June) as temperatures can exceed 42 degrees
Celsius, making long queues and walks uncomfortable.

**Q: Is the Navagraha Temple safe for solo female travellers?**

Yes, Ujjain is generally considered safe for solo pilgrims. The temple is well-visited
throughout the day with families and groups present at all times. As always, standard
precautions apply — dress modestly, carry your belongings safely, and avoid isolated areas
after dark.

**Q: Can I visit Navagraha Temple at night?**

The temple closes by 9:00 PM. For a special spiritual experience, visit during the
**Amavasya (new moon) night** when special abhishek ceremonies are conducted until
closing time. The serene atmosphere on the banks of the Shipra River after the evening
aarti is truly unforgettable.
