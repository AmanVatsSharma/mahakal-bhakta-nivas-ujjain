Long before Ujjain became the city of Mahakal in your travel plan, it was a city of teachers. **Sandipani Ashram Ujjain** is the reason: tradition holds that **child Krishna, his brother Balaram and his friend Sudama studied here** under **Guru Sandipani**, in the ashram of a city then called Avantika. For a pilgrim walking the Mahakal circuit, this is the stop where the mythology changes register — from the thunder of Shiva to the quiet of a schoolyard where a divine child once sat cross-legged, learning his letters.

The ashram lies just 2 to 3 km from the temple quarter where our dharamshala stands, 200 m from the Mahakal gate. It is the easiest big story in Ujjain to reach, and often the one families with children remember longest.

## The Story of Krishna, the Student

Every Indian child knows Krishna the flute-player, Krishna the charioteer, Krishna of Vrindavan. Fewer know **Krishna the student** — and that chapter happened here.

After the dangers of Mathura ended, tradition says, Krishna and Balaram were sent to **Avantika (Ujjain)** to study under the sage **Sandipani**. With them came **Sudama** — the poor brahmin boy who would one day walk to Dwarka to meet his old classmate with a handful of flattened rice, and be received like an emperor. The friendship that devotees hold up as the ideal of childhood love was forged in this ashram.

Two details of the story are told and retold here:

- The **speed of the learning** — local tradition says Krishna mastered the sixty-four arts in sixty-four days, the teacher finding in this student no need for a second lesson on anything.
- The **gurudakshina** — when training ended, Guru Sandipani asked not for gold but for the return of his son, lost to the sea. Krishna went to **Prabhasa** on the coast, defeated the demon Panchajana, and brought the boy back from the ocean. The guru's fee, in this telling, was a resurrection.

Stand at **Gomati Kund** with these stories in your head and the ashram stops being a monument. It becomes what pilgrims believe it is: a **5,000-year-old seat of learning**, still open.

## What You See at the Ashram

- **The shrines** — a small sanctum where Krishna, Balaram and Sudama are honoured together **as students**, with Guru Sandipani. The image of the three boys at their studies is unusual in Indian temple art, where Krishna is almost always the god and never the pupil; here, the roles reverse.
- **Gomati Kund** — the sacred tank, calm and green, ringed by stone. Devotees revere its waters, traditionally connected to the **Gomati river**, and many take a small bottle home.
- **The Naga idols** — the old serpent figures around the kund area are among the most photographed antiquities in Ujjain. Carved coiled and hooded, they guard the water in the old way, and worshippers offer milk and flowers to them even today.
- **The Saraswati kund area** — the ashram's water traditions are layered; ask the priests to point out the spots linked to the goddess of learning, the natural patron of a school.

The overall feel is more campus than temple: low buildings, open ground, shaded seating, and a rhythm of visiting families rather than rushing tour groups.

## Timings and the Best Slot

The shrines open in the morning and run with the usual **midday break** into the evening. As always in Ujjain, festival days move the clock — **verify timings locally** on your visit day.

- **Morning (8 AM to 11 AM)** is the best slot: cool, quiet, good light on the kund, and priests with time to tell the stories.
- **Avoid the school-trip hours** around midday in season, when busloads of children arrive — though if you travel with kids, that energy is exactly the point; watching schoolchildren take notes where Krishna supposedly did is its own blessing.

## How to Reach From the Mahakaleshwar Temple Quarter

## Tips for Pilgrims

- **Go before the sun climbs** — the open courtyard and kund side have little shade at noon.
- **Carry a small bottle** if you wish to take kund water home; the shops nearby sell clean sealed ones too.
- **Respect the Naga idols** — photography is usually fine, but do not climb on or touch the carvings more than devotees do.
- **Ask a priest for the story** — the narrative told aloud at the spot is the real attraction; a small dakshina is customary.
- **Pair it with ISKCON** — the Krishna trail in Ujjain runs Sandipani Ashram in the morning and the Radha Madan Mohan temple in the evening, a satisfying day for Vaishnavas.
